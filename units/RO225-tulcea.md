# RO225 · Tulcea County (Județul Tulcea), Romania

**UNIT** RO225 (NUTS-3) = Tulcea County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 8,499 km², seat Tulcea; towns Babadag, Isaccea, Măcin, Sulina; 46 communes. Northern Dobruja: the Danube Delta (~3,450 km² of channels, lakes and reed), the Razim–Sinoe lagoon complex, the Măcin and Niculițel hills, and the Babadag and Casimcea plateaus between the Danube and the sea. Historic components: the Ottoman kazas of Tulcea, Isaccea, Măcin and Babadag; the Romanian county of Tulcea from 1878, whose boundaries changed little; the Sulina mouth under the European Commission of the Danube (1856–1938). Pre-1948 figures are the county's own censuses; the INS current-territory series begins in 1948. Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: the Hamangia culture at its type site (Baia-Hamangia) and along the lagoon shores, the Danube bank and the Babadag valleys, passing into Gumelnița.
**Documented-era peak** Window 4, c. 200 CE: Troesmis (the 16 ha legionary fortress of legio V Macedonica, 107–163, with a 30–35 ha civil settlement, canabae and municipium), Noviodunum (base of the classis Flavia Moesica, municipium), Aegyssus, Arrubium, Dinogetia, Halmyris and Argamum, with the vici of their territories; band 9 at the lawyered top (range 7–9). Window 5, c. 400 (Ibida, Halmyris, legio II Herculia at Troesmis), ties. Census-grade peak: band 6 (population 1912–1948; GDP 1912).

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 8,499 km² (INS); the Delta, the lagoons and the Danube marshes (~4,200 km²) and the driest Casimcea plateau are excluded: usable lagoon shores, Danube terraces, the Babadag and Taița valleys and the Măcin piedmont 2,500–3,300 km².
2. Cultures at the moment: late Hamangia at Baia-Hamangia (the type site), Ceamurlia de Jos, Golovița, Sarichioi and the Isaccea bank, passing into Gumelnița A on the Danube (Luncavița tell, Carcaliu); the Hamangia cemeteries are the richest Chalcolithic burial record in Romania.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009), as for Constanța. Equivalence rule applied. → 5,000–9,900, reported 5–9.9k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: farming densities in the Delta and on the dry plateau; killed any county total above ~13,000 (>4/km² usable).
- Survives: 9,900 people means ~50–100 villages and hamlets of 100–200 along the lagoons, the Danube and the valleys at 4–7 km spacing; cropland need under 3% of the usable area; the lagoon fisheries carry the rest.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Deep score — Window 4, c. 200 CE (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Towns and forts 20–36k.
2. Countryside: the vici of the Troesmis and Noviodunum territories (vicus Novus, vicus Verobrittiani and the rest named in the inscriptions) on the Babadag plateau and the Danube bank at 5–8 per km² of usable land; 20–34k. County 40–70k.

Denominator: 190M at 200 CE (McEvedy & Jones).

Veto notes

- Killed: counting Tomis, Histria or Capidava (Constanța).
- Window 5 tie: c. 400, Scythia's northern cities (Ibida at 24 ha walled, Halmyris with its bishopric and martyrs' crypt, Noviodunum, Troesmis under legio II Herculia, Dinogetia, Aegyssus): 40–70k over 190M, multiplier 1.3–1.8 → the same 0.027–0.066%. c. 550: 30–55k over 185M, ×1.2–1.7 → 0.0195–0.0505% (band 7–9).

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 7.** Nameable chokepoint and command: the Sulina mouth, the Danube's maritime gate, cut and kept open by the European Commission of the Danube from 1856; the Isaccea crossing, the Danube's classic ford for Darius, the Goths and five Russian armies (1771–1877); the fleet base at Noviodunum and the legionary fortress of Troesmis; the Delta itself as a military barrier. Not 8: the Commission sat at Galați and the trade it served was Romania's, not a continent's.
- **Mind 6.** Nameable but regional: the Hamangia type site and the Thinker's culture; Argamum as the first attested Greek foundation of the western Pontus; the Niculițel martyrs' crypt (4th century), the earliest Christian martyrium in Romania; the Danube Delta Biosphere Reserve (World Heritage), Europe's largest wetland; the Sulina lighthouse and the Commission's hydrography. Natural heritage and archaeology, nothing of world intellectual scale, so 6.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is the Window 1 floor artefact (band 10 across the range). The documented-era record (c. 200 CE, tied c. 400) is band 9 only at the top of range and band 8 at the mid-range, half of it a density allocation. Census-grade band 6 is the ceiling from 1912 to 1948.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 (~5–6×); the c. 200 CE GDP figure is ~2.5–3×; the census-grade figures are 1–1.5×.
- **Boundary smearing.** Minimal: the county's boundaries have changed little since 1878; the 1899 and 1912 figures are the county's own censuses.
- **Double-count risk.** RO223 Constanța (Tomis, Histria, Capidava; the Hamangia and Babadag cultures span both counties), RO224 Galați (the Commission's seat; Barboși), RO221 Brăila (the Măcin arm), Ukrainian Odesa oblast (the Chilia arm and Ismail).
- **Citation flags.** The 2022 county GDP is an estimate from the county's recent national share (the only figure retrieved, 4.16 billion lei, is an older forecast); Troesmis's areas (16 ha fortress, 30–35 ha civil settlement) are from the Austrian Archaeological Institute's project; no printed Roman population estimate for northern Dobruja was retrieved.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1; band 8 held c. 110–450 CE on GDP; band 6 on population 1912–1956.
