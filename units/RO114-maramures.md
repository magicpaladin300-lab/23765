# RO114 · Maramureș County (Județul Maramureș), Romania

**UNIT** RO114 (NUTS-3) = Maramureș County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 6,304 km², seat Baia Mare; municipalities Baia Mare, Sighetu Marmației; towns Baia Sprie, Borșa, Cavnic, Dragomirești, Săliștea de Sus, Seini, Șomcuta Mare, Tăuții-Măgherăuș, Târgu Lăpuș, Ulmeni, Vișeu de Sus; 63 communes. The Baia Mare depression and the Gutâi–Țibleș volcanic range with its gold–silver veins, the Chioar and Lăpuș lands on the Someș and Lăpuș, and, north of the ridge, the historic Maramureș depression on the Tisza, Iza and Vișeu under the Rodna and Maramureș mountains. Historic components: the Romanian two fifths of Hungarian Máramaros (357,705 in 1910), the Nagybánya and Nagysomkút districts of Szatmár, and the Magyarlápos district of Szolnok-Doboka; the interwar counties of Maramureș (161,575 in 1930), Satu Mare (part) and Someș (part). Pre-1948 figures are allocations to the current territory; the INS current-territory series begins in 1948. Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: Tiszapolgár settlement of the Baia Mare depression, the Chioar and Lăpuș valleys and the Iza terraces; band 10 at the lawyered top (range 9–10).
**Documented-era peak** Window 8, c. 1450: the Baia Mare (Nagybánya) gold chamber and mint (1411) under the Hunyadis, when the town's mines yielded a third to a half of Hungary's gold (the mines produced around 400 kg of gold a year in the 14th century); band 8 at the lawyered top (range 7–8) on the extraction-grade multiplier (Ruling A). c. 1500 under the Thurzó–Fugger lease ties within range. Census-grade peak: GDP band 8 at the top of range (1850–1870), population band 7 (1787–1930).

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 6,304 km² (INS); the Rodna, Maramureș, Gutâi, Țibleș and Lăpuș mountains (~3,000 km²) and the high forests are excluded: usable depressions, terraces and hill valleys 2,200–2,800 km².
2. Cultures at the moment: Tiszapolgár and the late Iclod group in the Baia Mare depression (Oarța de Sus, Seini, Ardusat), the Chioar and Lăpuș, with the Iza and Tisza terraces of the northern depression thinly settled; salt at Ocna Șugatag and Coștiui.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied. → 4,400–8,400, reported 4.4–8.4k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: farming densities on the volcanic and crystalline ranges; killed any county total above ~11,000 (>4/km² usable).
- Survives: 8,400 people means ~40–85 villages and hamlets of 100–200 in the depressions and valleys at 5–8 km spacing; cropland need under 3% of the usable area.
- Band 10 holds only at the top of the range (0.168% population, 0.20% GDP); the mid-range is band 9.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Deep score — Window 8, c. 1450 (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Population 35–55k on the current territory: Baia Mare (Rivulus Dominarum), royal free town from 1347 with its mint (1411) and the mining towns of Baia Sprie and Cavnic, 4–6k; the Chioar and Lăpuș districts and the Maramureș knezial villages of the Iza and Vișeu (the voivodal families from which Bogdan of Cuhea went out to found Moldavia in 1359); Kubinyi's porta-based estimates for Máramaros, Szatmár and Közép-Szolnok, apportioned (the table could not be fetched; flagged).
2. GDP multiplier, extraction-grade (Ruling A, quantified source named): under the Hunyadis Nagybánya supplied about 32% of the kingdom's gold, and more than half under King Matthias; the mines produced around 400 kg of gold a year in the 14th century. At the gold florin's 3.5 g, 400 kg is ~110,000 florins a year, of the same order as the whole county's agrarian product (35–55k people at 2–3 florins), so the gold alone adds 0.8–1.6 to the agrarian base; with the mint and the silver, the multiplier relative to world is 1.8–3.0 on the Eastern Europe base of 0.88–1.0. The cap of 2.5 is exceeded under the extraction-grade exception, with the output figure as the named source.

Denominator: 350M at 1450 (rubric floors table).

Result: population 0.0100–0.0157% (band 6–7); GDP 0.0180–0.0471% (band 7–8; 0.032% at the mid-range of 45k × 2.4; 8 at the lawyered top).

Veto notes
- Killed: a multiplier above 3.0; the 400 kg figure is the 14th-century peak and the Hunyadi-era share is of a kingdom output of 1–1.5 tonnes, so the gold cannot exceed ~1.6× the agrarian product.
- Killed: counting Rodna (Bistrița-Năsăud) or the Hungarian-side Szatmár plain.
- Ties: 1411, the mint's foundation, 30–50k × 1.8–3.0 → 0.0154–0.0429%; c. 1500, the Thurzó–Fugger lease of the Nagybánya chamber (the "Common Hungarian Trade" of János Thurzó and Jakob Fugger, with Thurzó's water-extraction wheel built at Baia Mare), 45–70k over 425M × 1.76–2.5 (the quantified-source band on the Fugger accounts) → 0.0186–0.0412% (band 7–8).

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 6.** Nameable but regional: the Baia Mare gold–silver district and mint, with Kremnica one of the two chambers of Hungary's medieval gold; the Prislop pass and the Tisza salt road; the Maramureș voivodes and the 1359 foundation of Moldavia from Cuhea; the Sighet prison (1950–55); the Baia Mare non-ferrous complex of the twentieth century. No continental chokepoint.
- **Mind 7.** Nameable claims: the eight wooden churches of Maramureș (World Heritage), the Merry Cemetery of Săpânța; Elie Wiesel, Nobel Peace laureate, born at Sighet (1928); the Nagybánya artists' colony of Hollósy (1896), the cradle of modern Hungarian painting; the Sighet Memorial of the Victims of Communism. Regional institutions and one Nobel laureate, so 7.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** Band 10 holds only at the top of the Window 1 range (maximum usable area and the 3/km² density together); the mid-range is band 9. The documented-era record (c. 1450) is band 8 only at the top of range and band 7 at the mid-range; it rests on the extraction-grade multiplier for the gold chamber and on an apportioned population; c. 1500 and 1850–1870 tie at the top.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 (~5–6×); the c. 1450 GDP figure is ~2.5–3×; the 1787–1910 figures are 1–1.5×.
- **Boundary smearing.** Pre-1948 figures rebuild the polygon from the Romanian two fifths of Máramaros, two districts of Szatmár and one of Szolnok-Doboka, anchored on 1930 and 1948 (±15k, 5%); the Hungarian district tables and Kubinyi's rows could not be fetched (mek.oszk.hu and tti.abtk.hu are blocked).
- **Double-count risk.** RO115 Satu Mare (the Szatmár plain and Seini's hinterland), RO112 Bistrița-Năsăud (Rodna's silver and the Someș), RO116 Sălaj (the Someș bend), Ukrainian Zakarpattia (the northern three fifths of Máramaros).
- **Citation flags.** The gold figures (400 kg a year in the 14th century; 32% of the kingdom's gold under the Hunyadis, over half under Matthias) are the encyclopaedic figures from the mining literature; Kubinyi's porta estimates were not read.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1 at the top of range; band 8 at the top of range c. 1411–1520 and c. 1850–1870 on GDP; band 7 on population 1787–1930.
