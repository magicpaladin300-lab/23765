# RO122 · Brașov County (Județul Brașov), Romania

**UNIT** RO122 (NUTS-3) = Brașov County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968, in force 17 February 1968): 5,363 km², seat Brașov; municipalities Brașov, Codlea, Făgăraș, Săcele; towns Ghimbav, Predeal, Râșnov, Rupea, Victoria, Zărnești; 48 communes. Historic components, in 1876–1918 units: (a) all of Brassó County (1,499 km²; 101,199 in 1910) = Țara Bârsei / Burzenland with the Săcele Seven Villages; (b) Fogaras County (2,433 km²; 95,174 in 1910) including its Bran (Törcsvár) district, less the western Arpaș-district villages transferred to Sibiu County in 1968 (Arpașu de Jos and de Sus, Nou Român, Cârța, Cârțișoara, Porumbacu de Jos and de Sus, Scoreiu, Sărata, Colun; ≈13–15k people in 1910); (c) the Rupea (Kőhalom) district of Nagy-Küküllő County: the former Saxon seat of Rupea (619 km²; 21,387 in 1891) plus the Olt-valley communes of the old Felső-Fehér enclaves (Hoghiz, Racoș, Ormeniș, Augustin, Ungra, Hălmeag). Interwar (1925–1950): Brașov County, Făgăraș County and the Rupea plasă of Târnava Mare County. 1950–1968 the territory sat inside the much larger Stalin Region (Brașov Region from 1960), a polygon not used here. The 1940 Vienna Award line ran north of the polygon. Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: Precucuteni–Ariușd (Cucuteni A) and Petrești farmers of the Brașov and Făgăraș depressions.
**Documented-era peak** Window 9, c. 1500: the 1495 tax inventory and the 1503 customs register.
**Census-grade peak** Window 11, 1850 (population, Willcox floor); band 7 GDP held from 1850 to the present.
**WINNING PATHWAY** GDP in every window; the population pathway is practically tied in Window 1.

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 5,363 km² (INS). Mountains above roughly 800 m (Făgăraș, Piatra Craiului, Bucegi, Ciucaș, Perșani, Postăvarul, Piatra Mare, Întorsurii) cover some 40–45% of the polygon. Settled land: the Brașov Depression / Țara Bârsei (~1,500 km² with the Tărlung basin), the Olt terrace of Țara Făgărașului inside the county (~900 km²), and the Rupea–Hârtibaciu hill country (~700–900 km²) → 2,800–3,300 km² usable.
2. Cultures at the moment: Precucuteni III passing into the Ariușd (Cucuteni A) group in the Brașov Depression and the upper Olt (about 60 Precucuteni findspots are recorded for Transylvania as a whole, concentrated in the south-eastern depressions; the type site Ariușd lies just across the county line in Covasna), Petrești farmers in the Făgăraș and Rupea country, with early copper (Bodrogkeresztúr horizon) following. County inventory: Costea, *Repertoriul arheologic al județului Brașov*.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic farming-zone densities synthesized by Müller (2013, "from 1 to 8 million Europeans, 6000–2000 BCE") by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied (non-literate society, high end). → 5,600–9,900, reported 5–10k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian): farming, herding, copper-using communities against a world average that was still largely foragers.

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 1978 give 5M at 5000 BCE and 7M at 4000 BCE). No published world total below 5M exists for this date.

Veto notes
- Killed: applying Cucuteni–Trypillia giant-settlement densities (tens of persons per hectare over hundreds of hectares) to the Brașov Depression. Those sites are Ukrainian, 4100–3600 BCE, and nothing of the kind is attested in Transylvania.
- Killed: any county total above ~12,000 (>4/km² usable), which no regional synthesis supports, and any count of the mountain 40% at farming densities.
- Survives: 10,000 people means roughly 35–50 villages of 200–300 or 70–100 hamlets at 5–9 km spacing across the two depressions, consistent with the dozens of Ariușd-group findspots; cropland need at Neolithic yields is under 3% of the usable area.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, i.e. a third to a half of humanity in Europe. The score is a floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit, not a claim about Brașov.

## Deep score — Window 9, c. 1500 (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Brașov city ≈ 13,000 c. 1500: the taxpayer registers plus ~20% for the unregistered poor, as carried in the standard urban-history summaries; the largest town in Transylvania; about 6,000 in the mid-fifteenth century.
2. Present-day Burzenland ≈ 36,000 at the turn of the fifteenth–sixteenth centuries, from the 1495 tax inventory (Elekes & Szilágyi 2019, "The changing of settlement system and administrative features in Burzenland (Romania) from the 13th century to 1876"); this includes the city. Cross-check: Kubinyi (1996) counts 101,340 Transylvanian Saxons in the whole kingdom in 1495, of whom the Burzenland chapter was the second-largest block.
3. Țara Făgărașului inside the polygon: 12–20k, back-projected from the 1850 district figure (65,754) with the 2.3–2.8× Transylvanian growth between 1500 and 1850 and less the western villages now in Sibiu County; the seventeenth-century Făgăraș urbaria give the same order of magnitude.
4. Rupea seat 7–10k (21,387 in 1891, back-projected) and the Olt-valley enclaves 3–5k.
→ 55–75k.
5. GDP multiplier. Schedule band 2.0–2.5, "quantified fiscal/commercial documentation", named source: the Brașov vigesima (twentieth) customs registers of 1503 and 1529–1554 (*Quellen zur Geschichte der Stadt Kronstadt*, vols. 1–3; Manolescu 1965; Pakucs-Willcocks 2005, 2007). In 1503 the merchants of Târgșor alone traded almost 1,000,000 aspers with Brașov, second only to Câmpulung; Bucharest's merchants 182,823 aspers in 15 transactions; fish imports 5,285 poveri in 1503 and 8,713.5 poveri (888,500 aspers, 1.18 million kg) in 1545. Per C12 the premium is applied relative to the region, and the region's ratio to the world comes from Maddison: Eastern Europe $496 vs world $566 per head in 1500 (0.88); a maximalist puts Matthias-era Hungary at the world average (1.0). → 2.0–2.5 × 0.88–1.0 = 1.76–2.5.

Denominator: 425M at 1500 (McEvedy & Jones; the rubric floor). Biraben 460M, Durand's range 440–540M and Clark 427M are all higher; no cited total below 425M exists.

Result: population 0.0129–0.0176% (band 6–7); GDP 0.0228–0.0441% (band 7–8; 8 at the lawyered top).

Veto notes
- Killed: a Ruling A piercing above 2.5×. The registers' gross turnover (of the order of a few million aspers, roughly 100,000 florins at ~50 aspers to the florin) is at most a few tenths of the county's baseline agrarian product (55–75k people at ~10–12 florins of subsistence output each ≈ 600–900k florins), so even counting the whole turnover as value added the county-wide multiplier cannot exceed ~1.5 by arithmetic. The 2.0–2.5 used here is the rubric's schedule allowance for a documented entrepôt, not a computed ratio. A weighted city/village multiplier gives ~1.1–1.3 (the consensus column).
- Killed: nineteenth-century Saxon claims of 18–20k for sixteenth-century Brașov. The 1536 tax register and the plague toll of 1718–20 (4,500 dead, leaving under 11,000 in 1722) bound the city at 13k or less before 1700.
- Alternative moments: c. 1450 (40–55k over the 350M plague-trough floor; multiplier 1.4–1.76, entrepôt argued) gives 0.016–0.028% GDP; c. 1650 (60–80k over Willcox's 470M) gives 0.018–0.030%. Both stay under the 1500 top.

## Candidate — Window 4, c. 50 BCE (not fully lawyered)
Dacia under Burebista is put at 1.5–2.0 million in the standard reference summaries, a figure that rests on Strabo VII.3.13 (200,000 men under arms, later only 40,000) read at 1:8–1:10; Criton's 500,000 captives (via Lydus) would imply more but is emended by nearly everyone. The polygon is 1.6–1.8% of Burebista's Dacia by area and holds real Dacian centres (the fortresses at Racoș–Tipia Ormenișului, Augustin, Breaza and Crizbav; Cumidava at Râșnov after 106). Allocation 1.2–1.8% → 18–36k over the Deevey-deep floor of 137M at 50 BCE → 0.013–0.026% population, 0.011–0.032% GDP (×0.8–1.2), band 6–8. Physical veto: 2M over ~300,000 km² is ~7/km², below Gaul's attested Iron Age densities, so it survives; the numerator is an area allocation of a Dacia-wide figure and is therefore not adopted as the record.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 7.** (1) The pass gate between Transylvania and Wallachia: Bran castle (built 1377–1382 by the Brașov Saxons under Louis I's charter of 19 November 1377 to command the Rucăr–Bran road), the Timiș/Predeal pass (the main road and, from 1879, the Brașov–Ploiești railway), the Buzău pass (Tabla Buții); the Râșnov fortress; Cumidava on the Roman Carpathian frontier. (2) The Teutonic Order's Burzenland, 1211–1225: the Order's first territorial lordship and the organisational prototype of Prussia. (3) Twentieth-century strategic industry: IAR (Industria Aeronautică Română, Brașov, 1925; the IAR 80/81 fighters), the Făgăraș chemical and explosives combine (1922 by royal decree, Romania's first chemical combine, later Nitramonia), the Ucea/Victoria gunpowder works (1938, part-financed by Rheinmetall; the Stalin Chemical Plant after 1948), the Brașov truck and tractor works. A regional gate and national-scale industry, no global chokepoint or sole command, so no ≥8.
- **Mind 7.** (1) Johannes Honterus: the Brașov press (1539), *Rudimenta Cosmographica* (Brașov 1542, reprinted across Europe for a century), the humanist Gymnasium (1544) and the Reformation of the Transylvanian Saxons (*Reformationsbüchlein* 1543). (2) Deacon Coresi's press in Șcheii Brașovului (1557–1583): the earliest surviving Romanian-language printed books, foundational for literary Romanian; the Șchei Romanian school, attested 1495. (3) The Black Church (begun 1383), the largest Gothic church in Romania. Bran's "Dracula" tourism and the 1987 workers' revolt fall under the event-fame ban. Nationally foundational, not of world rank, so 7.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak rests on the rubric's 5M floor at 4500 BCE, exactly as for the seven Hungarian units; on a 7M floor it is 0.07–0.17%, band 10 either way. The documented-era peak is contested: c. 1450–1650 (entrepôt multiplier; c. 1500 is the record by top of range), 1850 (census-grade, Willcox floor), 1977 and 2022 PPP all reach band 7, and only the top of the c. 1500 range reaches band 8. The Dacian Window 4 allocation would reach 0.031% at its extreme top but is not adopted.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 (~4–5×) and the c. 1500 GDP figure (~3×, schedule multiplier against a computed ~1.1–1.3).
- **Boundary smearing.** The polygon unites three pre-1918 units. Every figure before 1948 is a reconstruction from component districts; the 1968 Fogaras→Sibiu transfer is estimated at 13–15k (1910), and the Rupea district figures at ±5k.
- **Double-count risk.** RO126 Sibiu (the western Făgăraș villages and the rest of the Saxon seats), RO123 Covasna (the Ariușd type site; the Burzenland prehistoric landscape and the Teutonic grant continue east of the county line), RO125 Mureș and RO124 Harghita (the rest of Târnava Mare). The Dacian allocation must sum to ≤100% of Dacia across Romanian units.
- **Not counted.** Starčevo–Criș and Linear Pottery settlement (before Window 1); tourism and ski transients at Poiana Brașov and Bran (C8).
- **Citation flags.** Willcox 1850 (C2) still awaits a page-level check; the 1503 register totals are given per town, not as a grand total.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1; band 7 (GDP) held c. 1450–1700 and again 1850–present; band 6 population held 1850–2011.
