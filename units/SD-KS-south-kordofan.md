# SD-KS · South Kordofan State (Wilayat Janub Kurdufan), Sudan

**UNIT** SD-KS (ISO 3166-2) = South Kordofan State. Scale flag (C14): an ADM1 unit of 79,470 km², fifteen to twenty times a NUTS-3 county; every share below carries the polygon-size caveat.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (state of 1994–2005, restored in its narrower form when West Kordofan was re-established in July 2013): 79,470 km², seat Kadugli; localities Kadugli, Dilling, Rashad, Abu Jibeiha, Talodi, Al-Abbasiya, Abu Karshola, Al-Quoz, Habila, Al-Leri, Al-Buram, Umm Dorein, Ar-Reif ash-Sharqi, Heiban, Dalami, Ghadir, Al-Tadamon. The Nuba Mountains massifs and inselbergs, the intermontane and surrounding clay plains, the Tegali (Taqali) hills of Rashad in the east, Jebel Qadir in the south-east. Historic components: the Nuba hill communities, the Taqali kingdom (oral tradition from c. 1530, documented from the 18th century, Mahdist conquest 1884, British client state 1889–1930s), the Turco-Egyptian and Mahdist Kordofan, condominium Kordofan Province (Nuba Mountains Province 1914–29), Kordofan Province (1956–74), South Kordofan (1974–94). The 2008 census and the 2010 recount counted the larger 2005–13 state (158,355 km²), from which the current territory is allocated. Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: the Nuba Mountains, the best-watered highland of the Sudanese Sahel, and their humid-period plains at the equivalence-rule densities (C14 highland band on the hills).
**Documented-era peak** Window 10, c. 1650: the Nuba hill communities and the Taqali kingdom in the Funj era (Spaulding's Sinnar sources; Krump 1701; Bruce's "Tegla" in 1772), before the 18th–19th-century slave raids thinned the hills; band 9 at the lawyered top (range 7–9, Willcox's 470M floor); the Taqali version c. 1790 (band 7–9, 9 at top of the population range) is a hair lower. Census-grade peak: band 8 (population 2024, 0.029–0.033%; CBS 2018 estimate 2,107,623 → 0.0277%, band 7). Contemporary GDP: band 5 (PPP, estimated).
**WINNING PATHWAY** Population (GDP tied in Window 1 at the 1.0 multiplier; population leads the hill-refuge record throughout).

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 79,470 km²: the Nuba Mountains massifs, inselbergs and their skirts (~20,000 km², granite hills to 1,400 m with perennial springs and today ~800 mm of rain, far wetter in the African Humid Period), and the intermontane and surrounding clay plains (~59,470 km²), wooded savanna at the moment.
2. Communities at the moment: Saharan–Sahelian pastoral Neolithic herder-cultivators and hill foragers; the archaeological reconnaissance of the Nuba Mountains (Taylor & Bieniada 2007) records grinding hollows, stone tools and rock art on the hills but no excavated Neolithic settlement, so the equivalence rule for a non-literate society applies at the high end of the regional density range (C14), with the Khartoum Neolithic and the Wadi Howar sequences as the regional anchors.
3. Density, equivalence rule, high end: 0.8–1.5 persons/km² on the hills (C14 best-watered highland band; the Nuba hills are the one highland of the Sudanese Sahel between the Nile and Jebel Marra), 0.2–0.4 on the plains (Hassan's savanna range). → 16–30k on the hills, 11.9–23.8k on the plains, 27.9–53.8k in all.
4. GDP multiplier 0.8–1.0 (herder-cultivator economy with cattle wealth and hill springs, no towns).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: a hill area above 20,000 km² (the massifs and their skirts, not the whole 48,000 km² Nuba Mountains region, much of which is plain); killed hill densities above 1.5/km²; killed a total above ~60,000.
- Survives: 53,800 people means ~400–550 villages, camps and hamlets of 100, with half on and around the hills at 5–8 km spacing, the pattern the Nuba hills show in every later period.
- Allocation flag: the whole figure is an allocation; rock art and grinding hollows anchor occupation, not numbers.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking along the Nile and across the humid-period Sahel gives 2–3 million people against a 5M world floor, roughly half of humanity; the Kordofan units (SD-GK, SD-KS, SD-KN) together claim ~1.0–2.2% of the world in this window, the highland band being the reason this unit is the gallery's largest Window 1 share.

## Deep score — Window 10, c. 1650 (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. The Nuba hill communities: some fifty hill groups speaking Kordofanian, Nilo-Saharan and other languages, terraced hill agriculture (sorghum, sesame), iron-working, cattle in the plains in the wet season; the hills had drawn in populations displaced from the Kordofan plains by the Arab pastoral migrations of Windows 8–10. The plains groups (Hawazma and other Baggara, arriving in the 17th–18th centuries), the Taqali kingdom in the eastern hills (Rashad), and the Ghulfan, Dilling and Nyimang hills in the north. Lawyered 200–400k: the hills were more populous before the slave raids of the 18th–19th centuries (the raids from Sennar, Kordofan's Musabba'at and Darfur, then the Turco-Egyptian razzias from the 1820s), the 1956 census found ~550–600k Nuba after that century and a half, and the Nuba Mountains' present carrying capacity (1.5–2.5M) shows the hills could hold far more; the consensus column keeps 130–190k.
2. GDP multiplier 0.6–1.0: Maddison's Africa ratio (~0.7 of world c. 1650) × role premium 0.85–1.4 relative to the region for Taqali's court and its trade in slaves, ivory and iron with Sennar and Kordofan (at the top) against raided, subsistence hill farming (at the bottom); no capital premium beyond Taqali's small court, no ledger.

Denominator: 470M at 1650 (Willcox 1931; C2 deep floor); McEvedy & Jones's 545M would lower the shares by 14%.

Result: population 0.043–0.085% (band 8–9); GDP 0.026–0.085% (band 7–9; 0.051% at the mid-range of 300k × 0.8, band 9; 9 at the lawyered top). c. 1790 on 858.8M (Taqali documented by Bruce as a Funj tributary, the kingdom at its height under its 18th-century makks; the hills 250–450k): 0.0291–0.0524% population (band 8–9), GDP ×0.6–1.0 → 0.0175–0.0524% (band 7–9). c. 1850 on Willcox's 1,091M (the Turco-Egyptian razzias; Taqali independent): 250–450k × 0.6–0.9 → 0.0229–0.0412% population, 0.0137–0.0371% GDP (band 6–8).

Veto notes
- Killed: a hill population above 400,000 in 1650 (the 1956 count after the raids is the ceiling's anchor); killed a multiplier above 1.0.
- Killed: counting the Hawazma lands of West Kordofan (SD-GK) or El Obeid's hinterland (SD-KN).
- Allocation flag: the whole figure is a reconstruction; no contemporary count of the Nuba exists before the 20th century.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 6.** Nameable regional command: the Taqali kingdom, the one Nuba state, tributary to Sennar and client to Britain; Jebel Qadir, where the Mahdi made his hijra in 1881 and won the battle of Jebel Gedir (December 1881) that turned a revolt into the Mahdiyya; the Nuba Mountains as the hill-refuge that no plains power fully controlled; the SPLA/SPLM-N insurgencies (1985–2002, 2011–) and the Nuba Mountains Ceasefire of 2002, the first international monitoring mission of Sudan's wars. Not 7: no capital of a large state.
- **Mind 6.** Nameable claims: the Kordofanian languages, the homeland branch of Niger-Congo in Greenberg's classification, a key node of African linguistics; Nadel's "The Nuba" (1947), a classic of anthropology; Nuba wrestling, body art and Leni Riefenstahl's "The Last of the Nuba" (1973), which made the Nuba world-famous; Nuba music and the Kadugli and Dilling schools. Regional institutions, so 6.

## Flags
- **Scale (C14).** An ADM1 state of 79,470 km²; at NUTS-3 scale its shares would be a fifteenth to a twentieth. Every band above is a state-level band; the all-time rank (the gallery's largest Window 1 population share) is a polygon-size and highland-band effect, flagged as such.
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is a pure Window 1 allocation with rock art and grinding hollows as its only anchor; the hill share depends on the 20,000 km² hill area and the 1.5/km² highland ceiling. The documented-era record (c. 1650) reaches band 9 only at the top of range (400k people and the 1.0× multiplier on Willcox's floor), band 8–9 at the mid-range; its date rests on Funj-era sources that mention the hills, not count them. The census-grade record is band 8 only at 2024 (an estimate including the SPLM-N-held hills) and band 7 on the CBS figures.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 (~5–7×); the c. 1650 GDP figure (~3.5×). The 2010 allocation is ~1.5×; the census-grade figures 1.0–1.2×.
- **Boundary smearing.** All pre-1994 figures are allocations from Kordofan Province and its districts; the 2008 and 2010 counts are of the larger 2005–13 state and are allocated to the current polygon.
- **Double-count risk.** SD-GK West Kordofan (the Hawazma and Messiriya plains, Lagawa's Daju), SD-KN North Kordofan (El Obeid's hinterland, the northern Nyimang hills), SD-NW White Nile (the Kosti line), South Sudan's Unity and Upper Nile (the southern Nuba and the Bahr al-Arab line).
- **Citation flags.** No pre-20th-century Nuba count exists (the 200–400k figure is a reconstruction flagged as such); the 1956 Nuba total is recalled, not retrieved; the 2010 recount total and the 2018 CBS figure come from press and Wikipedia; the 2024 population and all GDP shares are estimates.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 in Windows 1–2 (c. 4500–1300 BCE); band 9 only at the top of range c. 1550–1800; band 7 held 1900–2018 on the census-grade series, band 8 from c. 2020.
