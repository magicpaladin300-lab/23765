# SD-GK · West Kordofan State (Wilayat Gharb Kurdufan), Sudan

**UNIT** SD-GK (ISO 3166-2) = West Kordofan State. Scale flag (C14): an ADM1 unit of 111,373 km², twenty to thirty times a NUTS-3 county; every share below carries the polygon-size caveat.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (state of 1994–2005, re-established July 2013): 111,373 km², seat Al-Fulah; localities Al-Fulah, Babanusa, Muglad, Abyei (as administered by Sudan), Keilak, Lagawa, As-Salam, An-Nuhud, Ghubaysh, Wad Banda, Abu Zabad, As-Sunut, Al-Khuwei, Al-Idiyya. Dar Hamar on the qoz sands of the north (An-Nuhud, the gum arabic market), the Messiriya and Humr Baggara belt of the south (Muglad, Babanusa, Al-Fulah), the Daju hills of Lagawa, Lake Keilak, the Muglad Basin and the Heglig oil field. Historic components: western Kordofan under the Musabba'at and the Funj, under Darfur (1785–1821), Turco-Egyptian Kordofan (1821–83), the Mahdiyya (1883–98), condominium Kordofan Province (Dar Hamar and Dar Messiriya districts), Kordofan Province (1956–74), North and South Kordofan (1974–94, 2005–13). Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: the humid-period savanna of western Kordofan, between the Nile Neolithic and the Chadic basin, at the equivalence-rule savanna densities over a very large polygon.
**Documented-era peak** Window 11, c. 1790: western Kordofan under the Darfur sultanate's governors (1785–1821), on the El Fasher–El Obeid route of Browne's decade, with Dar Hamar's gum arabic and the Baggara cattle; band 8 at the lawyered top (range 6–8, the 858.8M deep floor). Census-grade peak: band 7 (population 1956–2024; CBS 2018 estimate 1,764,800 → 0.0232%; 2024 trend 0.0245–0.027%). Contemporary GDP: band 6 at the 2012 Heglig moment (PPP, estimated; extraction-grade component).

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 111,373 km²: the qoz sands of Dar Hamar and the northern plains (~55,000 km²), savanna in the African Humid Period with seasonal lakes; the Baggara clay plains and the Muglad Basin of the south (~56,000 km²), wooded savanna with Lake Keilak and the Bahr al-Arab's northern feeders.
2. Communities at the moment: Saharan–Sahelian pastoral Neolithic herders and fisher-foragers; no excavated sites inside the polygon are known to me, so the equivalence rule for a non-literate society applies at the high end of the regional density range (C14), with the Khartoum Neolithic and the Wadi Howar sequences as the regional anchors.
3. Density, equivalence rule, high end: 0.15–0.3 persons/km² on the northern qoz, 0.2–0.4 on the southern savanna (Hassan's Saharan–Sahelian Neolithic range, C14). → 8.3–16.5k north, 11.2–22.4k south, 19.5–38.9k in all.
4. GDP multiplier 0.8–1.0 (herder-forager economy with cattle wealth, no towns).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: densities above 0.4/km² anywhere in the polygon; killed a total above ~45,000.
- Survives: 38,900 people means ~300–400 camps and hamlets of 100 over 111,000 km², one per ~300 km², a mobile-herder landscape.
- Allocation flag: the whole figure is an allocation; no site list anchors it.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking along the Nile and across the humid-period Sahel gives 2–3 million people against a 5M world floor, roughly half of humanity; the Kordofan units (SD-GK, SD-KN, SD-KS) together claim ~0.6–1.6% of the world in this window.

## Deep score — Window 11, c. 1790 (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Western Kordofan under Darfur's governors (the sultanate conquered Kordofan from the Musabba'at in 1785 and held it to 1821): Dar Hamar's villages on the qoz (An-Nuhud's predecessors, the gum arabic and millet economy), the Messiriya and Humr Baggara (arrived in the 18th century), the Daju of Lagawa, the Shanabla and Bedeiria. Kordofan's whole population before 1821 lawyered at 400–700k (the Turco-Egyptian poll-tax rolls of the 1820s–30s, Pallme's 1838 observations of a populous province, and Reclus's post-famine 300,000 as the floor of the floor); the western third to half → 130–350k.
2. GDP multiplier 0.7–1.1: Maddison's Africa ratio (~0.7 of world c. 1790) × role premium 1.0–1.6 relative to the region for the gum arabic export belt (Kordofan gum reached Cairo and Europe via Darfur and Egypt), the cattle wealth of the Baggara and the Darfur–El Obeid route; no capital premium, no ledger.

Denominator: 858.8M at 1790 (C2 deep floor); McEvedy & Jones's 900M at 1800 would lower the shares by 5%.

Result: population 0.0151–0.0408% (band 7–8); GDP 0.0106–0.0448% (band 6–8; 0.0251% at the mid-range of 240k × 0.9, band 7; 8 at the lawyered top). c. 1750 on Willcox's 694M (the Musabba'at and Funj contest): 100–280k × 0.7–1.0 → 0.0144–0.0403% population, 0.0101–0.0403% GDP (band 6–8). c. 1850 on Willcox's 1,091M (Turco-Egyptian Kordofan; An-Nuhud founded as a market): 150–350k × 0.7–1.1 → 0.0137–0.0321% population, 0.0096–0.0353% GDP (band 6–8).

Veto notes
- Killed: a Kordofan above 700,000 before 1821; killed a western share above half; killed a multiplier above 1.1.
- Killed: counting El Obeid and Bara (SD-KN) or the Nuba hills (SD-KS).

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 6.** Nameable regional command: the Heglig oil field, Sudan's main source of oil after 2011 and the object of the Heglig Crisis of April 2012 (South Sudan's occupation, an inter-state war episode); the Abyei dispute and the Messiriya–Dinka Ngok line; Babanusa, the rail junction (1959–62) of the Nyala and Wau lines; the Baggara belt that supplied the Mahdiyya's and the 2000s' militias. Not 7: no capital, no state of its own.
- **Mind 4.** Nameable but local: the gum arabic knowledge economy of Dar Hamar (An-Nuhud's market), the Messiriya's oral and legal traditions, the University of West Kordofan (Al-Fulah). Not 5: no school or institution of more than regional reach.

## Flags
- **Scale (C14).** An ADM1 state of 111,373 km²; at NUTS-3 scale its shares would be a twentieth to a thirtieth. Every band above is a state-level band; the all-time rank is a polygon-size effect.
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is a pure Window 1 allocation with no site anchor. The documented-era record (c. 1790) reaches band 8 only at the top of range (350k people and the 1.1× multiplier on the deep floor), band 7 at the mid-range; c. 1650 would be a hair higher but is thinner. The census-grade record is band 7 throughout 1956–2024 and does not depend on the lawyering.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 (~5–8×); the c. 1790 GDP figure (~3×). The census-grade figures are 1.0–1.2×.
- **Boundary smearing.** All pre-1994 figures are allocations from Kordofan Province and its districts; the Abyei area is counted as Sudan administers it (disputed).
- **Double-count risk.** SD-KN North Kordofan (El Obeid, Bara, the eastern Hamar), SD-KS South Kordofan (the Nuba hills, Lagawa's eastern neighbours), SD-DE East Darfur (the Rizeigat–Messiriya line), South Sudan's Abyei and Unity (the Heglig/Panthou claim).
- **Citation flags.** No pre-1821 Kordofan population total was retrieved (the 400–700k figure is a reconstruction flagged as such); the 1956 district totals are recalled, not retrieved; the 2008 territory figure is a locality sum; the 2024 population and all GDP shares are estimates; Heglig's 2012 output and the oil share are from press reports.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 in Windows 1–2 (c. 4500–1300 BCE); band 8 only at the top of range c. 1650–1800; band 7 held 1956–2024 on the census-grade series.
