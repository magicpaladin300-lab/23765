# RO414 · Olt County (Județul Olt), Romania

**UNIT** RO414 (NUTS-3) = Olt County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 5,498 km², seat Slatina; municipalities Slatina and Caracal; towns Balș, Corabia, Drăgănești-Olt, Piatra-Olt, Potcoava, Scornicești; 104 communes. The lower Olt valley with its terraces, the Romanați plain west of it, the Boian plain's western edge east of it, the Danube frontage at Corabia and the Cotmeana piedmont in the north; plain or low piedmont throughout. Historic components: the interwar counties of Olt (seat Slatina; 183,595 in 1930) and Romanați (seat Caracal), merged in 1968 with small exchanges towards Dolj, Vâlcea, Argeș and Teleorman. 1950–1968 the territory was split between the Argeș and Oltenia regions (polygons not used). Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: the Vădastra–Gumelnița–Sălcuța transition on the Olt terraces and the Romanați plain.
**Documented-era peak** Window 4, c. 200 CE: Romula (Reșca), capital of Dacia Malvensis and the largest Roman city south of the Carpathians, with Sucidava and the Limes Alutanus; band 8–9 (10 at the extreme top). Census-grade peak c. 1899–1912, band 7.

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 5,498 km² (INS); plain and low piedmont throughout, with the Olt floodplain and the Corabia marsh the only unusable ground: usable 5,000 km².
2. Cultures at the moment: Vădastra (type site Vădastra inside the polygon, with its excised and white-encrusted pottery, c. 5300–4600 BCE) passing into Gumelnița–Sălcuța; Sucidava-Celei, Brebeni, Slatina, Ipotești and Drăgănești-Olt among the published Chalcolithic settlements on the Olt terraces.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied. → 10,000–15,000.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: any county total above ~20,000 (>4/km²); killed farming densities on the Olt floodplain and the Danube marsh.
- Survives: 15,000 people means ~75–150 villages and hamlets of 100–200 at 5–8 km spacing along the Olt, the Olteț, the Teslui and the Danube terrace; cropland need under 3% of the polygon.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Deep score — Window 4, c. 200 CE (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Romula-Reșca: the site covers more than 306 ha on the western Olt terrace, the largest Roman city south of the Carpathians, municipium under Hadrian, colonia under Septimius Severus, walled by Philip the Arab in 248, with two castra and a numerus of Syrian archers; the capital of Dacia Malvensis (University of Bucharest excavations; UNESCO tentative proposal). Built-up area × attested provincial densities (100–150 persons/ha over 100–200 ha of the enclosed and suburban ground) → 10–25k.
2. Sucidava-Celei at Corabia, the Danube bridgehead town (3–6k), the Limes Alutanus forts (Slăveni, Enoșești-Acidava, Romula's camps, Islaz) with their vici, and the rural villae and villages of the Olt corridor at 6–10/km² over the county (Roman Dacia at 650k–1.2M on ~100,000 km²; the Olt corridor among its densest settled strips) → 30–50k.
→ 45–80k c. 200 CE (40–70k at the Hadrianic moment c. 150).
3. GDP multiplier per C12: provincial capital premium 1.6–2.0 (the rubric's "imperial capital" band, argued for the seat of a province of ~30,000 km²) × the Roman Empire's per-capita ratio to the world 1.0–1.2 (Maddison: the Roman core above the world's $467 of 1 CE; a frontier province at or near the imperial mean) → 1.6–2.4.

Denominator: 190M at 200 CE (rubric floors table and McEvedy & Jones; Deevey's deep floor does not bind after 1 CE). At 150 CE the deep floor is 176M.

Result: population 0.0237–0.0421% (band 7–8); GDP 0.038–0.101% (band 8–9, 10 at the extreme top). The Hadrianic moment c. 150 gives 0.0228–0.0398% and 0.036–0.096%, the same bands.

Veto notes
- Killed: the whole 306 ha at urban densities (30–45k for Romula alone); the figure is the archaeological site with necropolises and extramural quarters, and no Danubian provincial city of this rank exceeded ~25k.
- Killed: applying the capital premium to the whole province's population; only the county's own population is counted, the premium standing for the court, garrison and market functions concentrated in it.
- Consensus: Romula 5–10k, county 25–35k, multiplier ~1.1 → 0.013–0.022%.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is the Window 1 floor artefact. The documented-era record (c. 200 CE) rests on a built numerator for Romula and Sucidava plus a density allocation for the countryside, and on the provincial-capital premium applied county-wide; band 10 appears only at the extreme top. The census-grade peak (1899–1912, band 7) is the uncontested alternative.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 (~4–5×) and the c. 200 CE GDP figure (~3–4×).
- **Boundary smearing.** All pre-1948 figures are reconstructions from two historic counties; the Romanați share of the 1930 total is ±15k.
- **Double-count risk.** RO411 Dolj and RO415 Vâlcea (the western Limes Alutanus forts and the Romanați remainder), RO317 Teleorman (the Limes Transalutanus and the Boian plain), RO411 Dolj again for the Sălcuța culture. The Getic allocation must sum to ≤100% of Dacia across Romanian units; the Roman provincial numerator must not be re-counted by Dolj or Vâlcea for Dacia Malvensis.
- **Citation flags.** No published population figure for Romula exists; the 2022 GDP is scaled from the OECD 2023 county value.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1; band 8–9 GDP held c. 120–250; band 7 census-grade held c. 1850–1956.
