# RO314 · Giurgiu County (Județul Giurgiu), Romania

**UNIT** RO314 (NUTS-3) = Giurgiu County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (created 1981 from the southern part of the former Ilfov County, i.e. the old Vlașca; confirmed 1997): 3,526 km², seat Giurgiu; municipality Giurgiu; towns Bolintin-Vale, Mihăilești; 51 communes. The Burnas plain and the Vlașca forest-steppe between the Argeș and the Danube, with the Danube frontage and islands at Giurgiu. Historic components: the central and southern plăși of the old Vlașca County (seat Giurgiu) and the Bolintin area of Ilfov. 1950–1981 the territory sat in the Bucharest Region and then Ilfov County (polygons not used). Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: Boian–Gumelnița settlement of the Vlașca plain and the Danube terrace (Pietrele, Tangâru, Bucșani).
**Documented-era peak** Band 7 at the lawyered top (range 6–7): 1912 on the census pathway (Giurgiu as Bucharest's Danube port) and c. 1650 on the Ottoman-raya port premium (Willcox floor). Population record 1930–1948 (band 6).
**WINNING PATHWAY** GDP in Window 1 (population tied); GDP in the documented era.

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 3,526 km² (INS); the Danube marsh and islands excluded: usable plain and terrace 2,800–3,200 km². The Vlașca forest was farmable interfluve, not steppe.
2. Cultures at the moment: Boian (Vidra phase) passing into Gumelnița A1. Pietrele-Măgura Gorgana on the Danube terrace, the German Archaeological Institute's reference excavation of a Gumelnița tell with its flat settlement, Tangâru and Bucșani-Pod are the published anchors; the Câlniștea and Neajlov valleys carry the rest.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied. → 5,600–9,600, reported 6–10k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: farming densities on the Danube marsh; killed any county total above ~13,000 (>4/km² usable).
- Survives: 10,000 people means ~50–100 tells and flat villages of 100–200 along the Danube, Argeș, Neajlov and Câlniștea at 4–7 km spacing, the attested Gumelnița spacing on the Danube terrace; cropland need under 3% of the usable area.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 6.** The Giurgiu–Ruse crossing, the Bucharest–Istanbul road's Danube gate: Ottoman fortress and raya 1420–1829, the Friendship Bridge of 1954 (the only Romanian–Bulgarian Danube bridge until 2013), and in 1940–44 the Danube terminal of the Ploiești pipeline through which Axis oil was shipped upriver, a nameable strategic function of the war years. No chokepoint of more than regional reach in peacetime, so 6.
- **Mind 5.** Pietrele-Măgura Gorgana as a reference excavation of the Lower Danube Chalcolithic; Romania's first railway (1869); Comana monastery. Nothing nameable above regional rank.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is the Window 1 floor artefact. The documented-era record reaches band 7 only at the top of range, at two moments (1912, c. 1650) built on different premises; the midpoints are band 6.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 only (~4–5×); the c. 1650 figure is ~2–3×.
- **Boundary smearing.** The county dates from 1981; every pre-1930 figure is a back-projection from the 1930 current-territory total.
- **Double-count risk.** RO322 Ilfov and RO321 Bucharest (the Ilfov remainder), RO317 Teleorman (the Vlașca plăși scored there), the Bulgarian bank (Ruse) which is not on the list.
- **Citation flags.** The 2022 GDP and the pre-1930 totals are inferred.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1; band 6 held c. 1850–1956.
