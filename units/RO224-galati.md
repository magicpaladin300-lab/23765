# RO224 · Galați County (Județul Galați), Romania

**UNIT** RO224 (NUTS-3) = Galați County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 4,466 km², seat Galați; municipalities Galați, Tecuci; towns Berești, Târgu Bujor; 61 communes. The Covurlui plateau between the Prut and the Bârlad, the Siret–Bârlad lowland around Tecuci, the Brateș lake basin and the Danube bank from the Siret mouth to the Prut mouth. Historic components: the whole of Covurlui county (210,006 in 1930) and the eastern three fifths of Tecuci county (Tecuci town, Nicorești, Ivești, Liești, Cosmești), with small exchanges towards Vrancea and Vaslui. Pre-1948 figures are allocations to the current territory; the INS current-territory series begins in 1948. Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: the Stoicani–Aldeni aspect (type site Stoicani inside the polygon) where Gumelnița meets Cucuteni on the Covurlui plateau and the Prut, Siret and Bârlad valleys.
**Documented-era peak** Window 12, 1870: Galați as Moldavia's free port (1837–83) and seat of the European Commission of the Danube (1856), the Danube's grain and cattle entrepôt with the Commission's navigation statistics as the quantified source; band 8 at the lawyered top (range 7–8); 1912 (0.021–0.030%) and 1850 tie within range. Population peak 1930, band 7 at the top of range.

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 4,466 km² (INS); the Brateș and Danube marshes (~300 km²) and the driest Covurlui steppe are excluded: usable plateau and valleys 3,200–3,800 km².
2. Cultures at the moment: Gumelnița A2 and the Stoicani–Aldeni aspect (Stoicani, Suceveni, Bălănești, Tecuci) with Cucuteni A on the Bârlad; the Prut and Siret terraces carry the densest site lists of southern Moldavia.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied. → 6,400–11,400, reported 6.4–11.4k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: farming densities on the marsh and the driest steppe; killed any county total above ~15,000 (>4/km² usable).
- Survives: 11,400 people means ~55–115 villages and hamlets of 100–200 along the Prut, Siret, Bârlad and the plateau streams at 4–7 km spacing; cropland need under 3% of the usable area.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Deep score — Window 12, 1870 (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Population 155–170k on the current territory: the 1859–60 Moldavian census for Covurlui (with Galați at ~26k) and the Tecuci share, carried to 1870 with Galați at 60–80k in its free-port boom (the city of 62,545 in 1899 had been swollen by the grain trade and the Commission; some contemporary counts gave 80,000 in the 1870s).
2. GDP multiplier. Moldavia's per-capita ~0.95 of world × schedule band 2.0–2.5 ("quantified commercial documentation with a named source": the European Commission of the Danube's annual navigation statistics and the Galați port returns; the French consul's 1870 report naming Galați the general contractor of Moldavia's grain exports to England, France and Italy) → 1.9–2.4 (C12). Galați was 40–50% of the polygon's population, so the premium is carried by the city itself.

Denominator: 1,271.9M at 1870 (Maddison; the rubric floor).

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 8.** Nameable command and chokepoint: the seat of the European Commission of the Danube (1856–1938), the first standing international river commission, which rebuilt the Sulina channel and policed the maritime Danube; the free port of 1837 and Moldavia's grain gate; the Galați steel combine (1966), the largest in south-eastern Europe; the Damen shipyard and the river-sea port at the Siret and Prut mouths; the Barboși castellum. Not 9: the chokepoint it administered lies downstream at Sulina (Tulcea).
- **Mind 6.** Nameable but regional: the Precista fortified church (1647); the Commission's hydrographic and legal corpus; the Stoicani–Aldeni type site; the Lower Danube University; Galați's Greek, Jewish and Italian merchant communities of the free-port age. Nothing of world scale, so 6.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is the Window 1 floor artefact. The documented-era record is a plateau: GDP band 7 from 1850 to 1912 with the 1870 and 1912 tops touching band 8; the choice of 1870 rests on the Commission's statistics qualifying for the quantified-source band. Population peaks in 1930 at the band 6–7 line.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 only (~5×); the 1850–1912 figures are 1.3–2×.
- **Boundary smearing.** Pre-1948 figures rebuild the polygon from Covurlui and three fifths of Tecuci county (±10k, 3–4%); the 1870 Galați city figure is itself a range (60–80k).
- **Double-count risk.** RO221 Brăila (the twin port across the Danube, the shared grain hinterland), RO225 Tulcea (Sulina and the Commission's works), RO226 Vrancea (Tecuci county's western two fifths; Poiana), RO216 Vaslui (the Bârlad's grain).
- **Citation flags.** The 2022 county GDP is an estimate from the county's recent national share (no 2022 figure retrieved); the 1859–1912 county totals are from the census summaries; the 1870 Galați population range mixes census and contemporary counts.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1; band 7–8 held c. 1840–1914 on GDP; band 7 on GDP again c. 1970–1989; population band 6–7 1930–1977.
