# RO315 · Ialomița County (Județul Ialomița), Romania

**UNIT** RO315 (NUTS-3) = Ialomița County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (the northern half of the old Ialomița County after the 1981 creation of Călărași, plus the Urziceni–Fierbinți area of the former Ilfov; confirmed 1997): 4,453 km², seat Slobozia; municipalities Slobozia, Fetești, Urziceni; towns Amara, Căzănești, Fierbinți-Târg, Țăndărei; 59 communes. The Bărăgan steppe's core, the Ialomița valley and the Borcea arm of the Danube at Fetești with the northern part of the Ialomița Great Island (marsh until the 1960s drainage). Historic components: the Slobozia, Fetești and Urziceni plăși of Ialomița and Ilfov; the old county's seat was Călărași until 1952. 1950–1968 the territory sat in the Bucharest and Ialomița regions (polygons not used). Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: Boian–Gumelnița settlement of the Ialomița terrace and the Danube levees (Bordușani-Popină).
**Documented-era peak** Band 6 on a plateau from 1912 to 1956; population record 1930, GDP record 1912.
**WINNING PATHWAY** GDP in Window 1 (population tied); population in the documented era.

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 4,453 km² (INS). The Bărăgan interior was open steppe and the Great Island marsh, so the Chalcolithic settlement zone is the Ialomița terraces, the Danube–Borcea levees and the Mostiștea headwaters: 1,800–2,400 km² usable.
2. Cultures at the moment: Boian–Gumelnița. Bordușani-Popină on the Great Island, a Gumelnița tell excavated with Hârșova as the reference pair of the Lower Danube floodplain, and the Ialomița-terrace sites (Căzănești, Slobozia, Dridu's later type site) are the anchors.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied. → 3,600–7,200, reported 4–7k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: farming densities on the Bărăgan steppe and the marsh (another 2,000–2,600 km²), which would add 4–8k with no site evidence behind it.
- Killed: any county total above ~10,000 (>4/km² usable).
- Survives: 7,000 people means ~35–70 tells and villages of 100–200 along two river corridors at 5–8 km spacing; cropland need under 3% of the usable area.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 5.** The Fetești bridgehead of the Saligny bridges (1895), Romania's only rail link to Constanța and the sea until 1987 (the main span at Cernavodă is Constanța's); the Bărăgan as the deportation ground of 1951 (event). A shared national link, so 5.
- **Mind 4.** Bordușani as a reference Gumelnița tell; Dridu as the type site of the eighth–eleventh-century culture; the Amara spa. Nothing nameable above regional rank.

- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 only (~4–5×).

- **Double-count risk.** RO312 Călărași (the old county's southern half and the Great Island's southern part), RO322 Ilfov (the Urziceni–Fierbinți area), RO223 Constanța (the Cernavodă span and Hârșova), RO221 Brăila (the Bărăgan's north-east).
