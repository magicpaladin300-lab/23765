# RO413 · Mehedinți County (Județul Mehedinți), Romania

**UNIT** RO413 (NUTS-3) = Mehedinți County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 4,933 km², seat Drobeta-Turnu Severin; municipalities Drobeta-Turnu Severin, Orșova; towns Baia de Aramă, Strehaia, Vânju Mare; 61 communes. The Danube from the Cazane gorge and the Iron Gates to the Jiu confluence country, the Mehedinți plateau and mountains in the north-west, the Motru and Topolnița valleys and the Danube terraces around Vânju Mare. Historic components: the Wallachian and interwar county of Mehedinți (5,320 km²; 303,878 in 1930) less the Baia de Aramă–Motru area now in Gorj, plus Orșova and the Cazane bank from Severin county. Pre-1948 figures are allocations to the current territory; the INS current-territory series begins in 1948. Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: Sălcuța settlement of the Danube terraces, the Mehedinți plateau and the Motru and Topolnița valleys.
**Documented-era peak** Window 5, c. 250 CE (tied by Window 4, c. 200): Drobeta, the bridgehead city of Trajan's bridge (103–105), municipium under Hadrian (121, with 14,000 inhabitants) and colonia under Septimius Severus (193), covering 60 ha with almost 40,000 inhabitants by the mid-3rd century in the local literature, with Dierna (Orșova), the two customs stations and the Danube limes; band 9 at the lawyered top (range 8–9). Census-grade peak: band 7 (population 1850–1912; GDP 1899–1912).

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 4,933 km² (INS); the Mehedinți mountains and the karst plateau's barren tops (~1,100 km²) and the Danube marshes are excluded: usable terraces, valleys and plateau 3,300–3,800 km².
2. Cultures at the moment: Sălcuța (the Oltenian facies of the Gumelnița complex) on the Danube terraces (Ostrovul Corbului, Ostrovul Șimian) and the Motru and Topolnița valleys, with Vinča contacts across the river; the Iron Gates had carried sedentary communities since the Schela Cladovei Mesolithic (7th millennium BCE), which lies before Window 1.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied. → 6,600–11,400, reported 6.6–11.4k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: farming densities on the Mehedinți mountains and the bare karst; killed any county total above ~15,000 (>4/km² usable).
- Survives: 11,400 people means ~55–115 villages and hamlets of 100–200 along the Danube, Motru, Topolnița and Blahnița at 4–7 km spacing; cropland need under 3% of the usable area.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 8.** Nameable chokepoint of continental scale: the Iron Gates, the Danube's great defile and the only break in Europe's river highway, regulated by the Sip channel (1896) and dammed by the Iron Gates I plant (1972, 2,100 MW, then Europe's largest hydroelectric station); Trajan's bridge (103–105), the crossing that opened Dacia; the Roman customs stations of Drobeta and Dierna; the Severin fortress of the Hungarian banate; the Severin shipyard (1851); the Romag heavy-water plant that supplies Cernavodă. Not 9: the Danube's traffic is European, not global.
- **Mind 7.** Nameable claims: Trajan's bridge by Apollodorus of Damascus, 1,135 m long, the longest arch bridge in the world for over a thousand years, pictured on Trajan's Column; the Iron Gates Mesolithic (Schela Cladovei, Ostrovul Corbului), with Lepenski Vir opposite, among the earliest sedentary communities in Europe (7th millennium BCE); the Iron Gates Region Museum and the Roman and medieval remains at Drobeta. Engineering and archaeology of world reference, but no school or institution of lasting intellectual output, so 7.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is the Window 1 floor artefact. The documented-era record (c. 200–250 CE) is band 9 only at the top of range and band 8 at the mid-range; it rests on the local-monograph figure of ~40,000 for Drobeta, which the consensus halves. Census-grade band 7 is held on population 1850–1912 and on GDP 1899–1912.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 (~5×); the c. 250 CE GDP figure is ~2.5×; the census-grade figures are 1.1–1.5×.
- **Boundary smearing.** Pre-1948 figures rebuild the polygon from the Wallachian and interwar Mehedinți less the Baia de Aramă–Motru area, plus Orșova (±15k, 5%); the Baia de Aramă transfer is shared with RO412 Gorj.
- **Double-count risk.** RO412 Gorj (the Motru valley and Baia de Aramă), RO422 Caraș-Severin (the Cazane's upper gorge, Dierna's hinterland), RO414 Olt (Sucidava and the Constantinian bridge), Serbian Bor (Pontes and the bridge's southern end; Lepenski Vir).
- **Citation flags.** Drobeta's 14,000 (121) and ~40,000 (mid-3rd century) are the Wikipedia figures from the city's monographs; the 2022 county GDP is an estimate carried from the 2018 figure (7.1 billion lei); the 1850–1912 totals are reconstructions from the census summaries.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1; band 8–9 held c. 190–270 CE on GDP; band 7 on population c. 1850–1912.
