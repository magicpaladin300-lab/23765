# RO112 · Bistrița-Năsăud County (Județul Bistrița-Năsăud), Romania

**UNIT** RO112 (NUTS-3) = Bistrița-Năsăud County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 5,355 km², seat Bistrița; municipality Bistrița; towns Beclean, Năsăud, Sângeorz-Băi; 58 communes. The Rodna, Bârgău, Călimani and Țibleș mountains in the north and east (~35–40%), the Someșul Mare valley and the Bistrița hills, and the northern edge of the Transylvanian Plain. Historic components: Beszterce-Naszód County (4,014 km²; 127,843 in 1910), i.e. the Saxon Nösnerland with Bistrița and the Romanian Năsăud border-regiment district, plus the Beclean (Bethlen) district of Szolnok-Doboka County on the lower Someșul Mare; the interwar Năsăud County. 1950–1968 the territory sat in the Rodna, then Cluj, region (polygons not used). Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: Iclod-group and Petrești settlement of the Someșul Mare valley and the Transylvanian Plain's northern hills.
**Documented-era peak** Window 9, c. 1550: Bistrița's Moldavian trade with the town's customs registers as named source; band 7 at the lawyered top (range 6–7), tied by 1850 on Willcox's floor. Population record 1850–1930 (band 6).

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 5,355 km² (INS); the mountain north and east is excluded: usable valley, hill and plain land 3,000–3,500 km².
2. Cultures at the moment: the Iclod group of the late Neolithic–early Chalcolithic Someș basin and Petrești painted ware, with Tiszapolgár copper following; the Someșul Mare terraces and the Transylvanian Plain's northern valleys carry the known findspots (Fântânele, Șieu, Beclean, Bistrița).
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied. → 6,000–10,500, reported 6–10k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: farming densities on the mountain two fifths; killed any county total above ~14,000 (>4/km² usable).
- Survives: 10,000 people means ~50–100 villages and hamlets of 100–200 along the Someșul Mare, Șieu and Bistrița valleys at 5–8 km spacing; cropland need under 3% of the usable area.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Deep score — Window 9, c. 1550 (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Bistrița (Nösen), a free royal town from 1330 with a fifteen-day annual fair from 1353, 4–6k people in the mid-sixteenth century, the smallest of the great Saxon towns but with markets throughout Moldavia and craftsmen travelling east; the Nösnerland's Saxon villages 10–14k; the Romanian villages of the Someș, Șieu and Năsăud valleys 15–22k; the Beclean district 5–8k → 35–50k.
2. GDP multiplier. Schedule band 2.0–2.5, "quantified fiscal/commercial documentation", named source: the Bistrița customs registers and town accounts of the sixteenth century, used with Brașov's and Sibiu's by Pakucs-Willcocks (*Transylvania and its International Trade, 1525–1575*) to measure the volume and value of the Moldavian and Ottoman trade. Per C12 the premium is applied relative to the region and multiplied by Maddison's Eastern Europe ratio to the world (0.88–1.0) → 1.76–2.5. The town is 12–15% of the county, below Brașov's fifth, so the top of the band is thin.

Denominator: 485M at 1550 (McEvedy & Jones interpolation).

Result: population 0.0072–0.0103% (band 6); GDP 0.0127–0.0258% (band 6–7; 7 at the lawyered top).

Veto notes
- Killed: a Ruling A piercing above 2.5×; Bistrița's registered trade was a fraction of Brașov's and a few tenths of the county's agrarian product at most.
- Killed: the Rodna silver as an extraction numerator (Ruling B). Rodna was a German mining town with its own law-book c. 1270, destroyed by the Mongols in 1241 and worked again by Bistrița patricians in 1440–60, but no registered output survives; at a few hundred kilograms of silver a year the value is far below 0.001% of world GDP in any case.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is the Window 1 floor artefact. The documented-era record is split between c. 1550 (schedule premium on a town that is an eighth of the county) and 1850 (Willcox floor); both reach band 7 only at the top of range.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 only (~4–5×); the 1550 figure is ~2–3×.
- **Boundary smearing.** The Beclean district's transfer from Szolnok-Doboka; pre-1930 figures are reconstructions.
- **Double-count risk.** RO113 Cluj (the Szolnok-Doboka remainder and the Iclod type site), RO125 Mureș (the Transylvanian Plain), RO114 Maramureș (the Rodna massif's north side), RO215 Suceava (the Bârgău pass's far side).
- **Citation flags.** No read figure for sixteenth-century Bistrița's population or trade totals; the 2022 GDP is inferred from the county's share.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1; band 6–7 GDP held c. 1450–1700 and 1850; band 6 population held 1850–1948.
