# SD-NO · Northern State (Wilayat ash-Shamaliyya), Sudan

**UNIT** SD-NO (ISO 3166-2) = Northern State. Scale flag (C14): an ADM1 unit of 348,765 km², the largest in Sudan and fifty to seventy times a NUTS-3 county; every share below carries the polygon-size caveat, and the Window 1 figure is dominated by the desert allocation.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (state of 1994): 348,765 km², seat Dongola; localities Dongola, Wadi Halfa, Delgo, Al-Burgaig, Ad-Dabbah, Merowe, Al-Golid. The Nile from the Egyptian border at Wadi Halfa through the Second and Third Cataracts, the Kerma basin and the Dongola Reach to the Fourth Cataract at Merowe, with the Nubian Desert east of the river and the Laqiya–Wadi Howar desert west of it. Historic components: Lower and Upper Nubia's heartland (the A-Group, Kerma, Napatan Kush, Makuria), the Ottoman and Funj Dongola, the Turco-Egyptian and condominium Dongola and Halfa provinces, Northern Province (1956–94). Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: the Kadruka and pre-Kerma Neolithic of the Kerma basin and the Dongola Reach on the Nile, with the humid-period desert at the equivalence-rule densities.
**Documented-era peak** Window 3, c. 700 BCE: Napata at Jebel Barkal, royal and religious seat of the Kingdom of Kush under the 25th Dynasty, when Piye, Shabaka, Shebitku and Taharqa ruled Egypt from Nubia (c. 744–656 BCE), with Kawa, Pnubs (Kerma), Sanam and the royal cemeteries of El-Kurru and Nuri; band 10 (open-top extension 10–13) on GDP, the gallery's highest documented-era share. Census-grade peak: band 6 (population 1956–2024).

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 348,765 km²: the Nile floodplain and terraces from Wadi Halfa to the Fourth Cataract, ~800 km of river with a usable strip of 6,000–8,000 km² (the Kerma basin alone is ~50 km long and 10 km wide); the western desert (~200,000 km²) with the lower Wadi Howar and the Laqiya and Selima depressions; the Nubian Desert east of the river (~140,000 km²).
2. Communities at the moment: the Neolithic of the Kerma basin (the Kadruka cemeteries, c. 4700–4000 BCE, with hundreds of burials and cattle bucrania; the pre-Kerma that follows), the Khartoum Variant and Abkan on the Second Cataract, and the Leiterband herders of Wadi Howar; the Nile strip is among the best-documented Neolithic landscapes in Africa, the desert much less so.
3. Density, equivalence rule, high end: 2–4 persons/km² on the Nile strip (a herder-cultivator floodplain below the Predynastic Egyptian 10–30/km²), 0.03–0.08 on the western desert in the African Humid Period and 0.01–0.05 on the Nubian Desert (the drier end of the Saharan Neolithic range, C14). → 12–32k on the river, 7–20k in the deserts, 19–52k in all.
4. GDP multiplier 0.8–1.1 (riverine herder-cultivators with cattle wealth).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: Egyptian floodplain densities on the Nubian strip; killed any desert density above 0.08/km²; killed a total above ~60,000.
- Survives: 52,000 people means ~250–500 villages, camps and hamlets of 100, two thirds of them on the river at 2–4 km spacing; the Kadruka cemeteries and the Kerma-basin site lists support a dense riverine population, the desert figure rests on Wadi Howar's survey record.
- Allocation flag: two fifths of the numerator is a desert allocation across 340,000 km² at densities of a few persons per 100 km².

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking along the whole Nile from the Delta to the Fourth Cataract and across the humid-period Sahara gives 2–3 million people against a 5M world floor, roughly half of humanity; the floor artefact is at its most extreme for this, the largest polygon in the gallery.

## Deep score — Window 3, c. 700 BCE (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Built numerators: Napata, the royal and religious capital at Jebel Barkal with the Amun temple (B 500), the palace and the town and the temple-and-treasury complex at Sanam across the river, 8–15k with its satellites; the royal cemeteries of El-Kurru (Piye, Shabaka, Shebitku) and Nuri (Taharqa) with their pyramid-building workforce; Kawa (Gematon), rebuilt by Taharqa, 3–6k; Pnubs (Kerma/Tabo) and the Kerma-basin towns 5–10k; the Dongola Reach's and the Second Cataract's river towns 5–10k. Towns 21–41k.
2. Countryside: the Nile strip's villages at 6–20 per km² of floodplain (6,000–8,000 km²), the Kushite heartland's granary, 36–130k; the Bayuda and desert herders 3–9k. County 60–180k (the upper reading of the Napatan Dongola Reach, the kingdom's core while its kings reigned in Memphis and Thebes).
3. GDP multiplier 1.6–2.5: the Egypt–Nubia region's per-capita level c. 700 BCE (~1.2–1.3 of a world dominated by agrarian Asia) × role premium 1.3–2.0 relative to the region for the capital of the state that ruled Egypt, with Egypt's tribute, the Amun temple's estates and the gold of the Nubian Desert flowing to Napata ("capital/entrepôt argued"; the sources are royal stelae and Assyrian annals, not ledgers, so the cap of 2.5 is not exceeded).

Denominator: 80M at 700 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 50M at 1000 BCE, 100M at 500 BCE).

Result: population 0.075–0.225% (band 9–10); GDP 0.12–0.56% (band 10; 0.29% at the mid-range of 120k × 2.0, extension 12; extension 13 at the lawyered top). c. 650 BCE, after the loss of Egypt (656): 60–180k over 85M × 1.5–2.3 → 0.106–0.487% (band 10, extension 10–12).

Veto notes
- Killed: a county above ~200,000 (the Dongola Reach cannot have carried more than 25/km² of floodplain); killed a multiplier above 2.5 (no quantified tribute total; Ruling A not invoked).
- Killed: counting Egypt's own population or product (the kings ruled it, the polygon did not produce it), or Meroe and the Butana (River Nile state).
- Ancient-source grounding: Piye's Victory Stela (c. 727 BCE), Taharqa's Kawa stelae, the Assyrian annals of Esarhaddon and Ashurbanipal, Herodotus (II.137–139, Sabacos) and 2 Kings 19:9 (Tirhakah) document the kingdom and its seat; none gives a population, so the numerator is a built-plus-allocation figure.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 8.** Nameable command: Napata, seat of the Kushite kings who ruled Egypt as the 25th Dynasty (c. 744–656 BCE), the only time Egypt was governed from a capital south of it; Kerma, Egypt's rival in the 17th century BCE; Old Dongola and the Baqt treaty (652), which fixed the Islamic–Christian frontier on the Nile for six centuries; Wadi Halfa, the Nile gate to Egypt (the 1897 railway, Kitchener's base); the Merowe Dam (2009), Sudan's largest. A 9 is arguable on the 25th Dynasty alone; the peg stays 8 because the command of Egypt lasted ninety years and was exercised from Memphis as much as from Napata.
- **Mind 8.** Nameable claims of world reference: Jebel Barkal, the "Pure Mountain", the southern seat of Amun in Egyptian religion (World Heritage with Napata's sites); the royal pyramids of El-Kurru and Nuri and Taharqa, named in the Bible and Herodotus; Kerma's deffufas, the largest mud-brick monuments in Africa, and its 30,000-tomb cemetery; the Kadruka Neolithic cemeteries; Old Nubian, the written language of Makuria's churches, among the oldest written African languages. Two ancient capitals and a written language carry the peg to 8.

## Flags
- **Scale (C14).** An ADM1 state of 348,765 km²; at NUTS-3 scale (a Nile reach of 5,000 km²) its Window 1 share would be about a tenth and its ancient shares a third to a half. Every band above is a state-level band.
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak stacks an attested Nile Neolithic with a desert allocation of a few persons per 100 km² over 340,000 km²; the Nile strip alone gives 0.24–0.64% (band 10, extension 11–13). The documented-era record (c. 700 BCE) is extension 13 only at the top of range (180k people and the 2.5× multiplier together), extension 12 at the mid-range; Classic Kerma c. 1700 BCE and the A-Group c. 3300 BCE tie within range on larger floors' complements, and Old Dongola c. 900 touches band 10 at the top. The census-grade series is a flat band 6.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 (~7–8×); the c. 700 BCE GDP figure (~4–5×); Kerma (~4×); Old Dongola (~3×). The contemporary figures are 1.0–1.05×.
- **Boundary smearing.** Pre-1956 figures are allocations from Dongola and Halfa provinces; the ancient figures are allocations of the Dongola Reach's floodplain with built town numerators; the Aswan reservoir removed Wadi Halfa's old town and 50,000 Nubians in 1964.
- **Double-count risk.** SD-NR River Nile (Meroe, the Fourth to Sixth Cataract, Abu Hamad's gold), Egypt's Aswan Governorate (Lower Nubia, Qustul, Qasr Ibrim), SD-DN North Darfur (Wadi Howar's upper course), SD-KN North Kordofan (the Bayuda's west).
- **Citation flags.** Kerma's "at least 10,000" (Bonnet) and the 30,000 tombs are the published figures; no Napatan or Makurian population estimate was retrieved, so those numerators are built-plus-allocation; the 2024 population and the GDP shares are estimates.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 in Window 1 and continuously from c. 3300 BCE to c. 300 BCE on the lawyered figures (extension 10–13), touching band 10 again c. 550–900; band 6 on population since 1956.
