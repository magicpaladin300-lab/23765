# RO423 · Hunedoara County (Județul Hunedoara), Romania

**UNIT** RO423 (NUTS-3) = Hunedoara County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 7,063 km², seat Deva; municipalities Deva, Hunedoara, Brad, Lupeni, Orăștie, Petroșani, Vulcan; towns Aninoasa, Călan, Geoagiu, Hațeg, Petrila, Simeria, Uricani; 55 communes. The Mureș corridor (Deva, Simeria, Orăștie), the Strei and Hațeg basin, the Cerna valley, the Brad basin on the Crișul Alb, the upper Jiu valley, and the Retezat, Parâng, Șureanu (Orăștie), Poiana Ruscă and Metaliferi mountains. Historic components: the Hungarian county of Hunyad (7,809 km²) less the Cugir–Șibot corner (Alba since 1968); the interwar Hunedoara county (7,695 km²). Pre-1948 figures are allocations to the current territory. Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: the Turdaș culture (type site Turdaș on the Mureș, with its incised signs) on the Mureș, Strei and Cerna valleys and in the Hațeg and Brad basins; band 10 at the lawyered top (range 9–10).
**Documented-era peak** Window 4, c. 200 CE: Colonia Ulpia Traiana Sarmizegetusa (20–30k; the capital of Roman Dacia, seat of the provincial council and the financial procurator), Micia, Germisara, Aquae and the Roman gold mines of Ruda-Brad; band 10 at the lawyered top (range 9–10; open-top extension 11 at the very top). The Dacian royal capital at Sarmizegetusa Regia (c. 50 BCE–106 CE) ties within range on the ancient-source numerator. Census-grade peak: GDP band 8 (1910), population band 7 (1787–1930).

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 7,063 km² (INS); the mountains (~4,000 km²) and the forested high plateaus are excluded: usable Mureș corridor, Strei and Hațeg basin, Cerna valley, Brad basin and the lower Jiu 2,200–3,000 km².
2. Cultures at the moment: late Turdaș (Vinča C) on the Mureș at Turdaș, Orăștie and Deva, with Petrești and Tiszapolgár contacts in the Hațeg and Brad basins; Turdaș's incised signs (Torma's collection) are the polygon's claim to the proto-writing debate, with the Tărtăria tablets 30 km downstream in Alba.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied. → 4,400–9,000, reported 4.4–9k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: farming densities on the Retezat, Parâng and Șureanu; killed any county total above ~12,000 (>4/km² usable).
- Survives: 9,000 people means ~45–90 villages and hamlets of 100–200 on the valleys and basins at 4–7 km spacing; cropland need under 3% of the usable area.
- Band 10 holds only at the top of the range (0.18% population, 0.216% GDP); the mid-range is band 9.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Deep score — Window 4, c. 200 CE (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Built numerators: Colonia Ulpia Traiana Augusta Dacica Sarmizegetusa, 30–33 ha walled with extensive extramural quarters, 20,000–25,000 or 25,000–30,000 inhabitants in the literature, the capital of Dacia (of Dacia Apulensis after Hadrian), seat of the concilium trium Daciarum, the imperial cult and the financial procurator, metropolis under Severus Alexander; Micia (Vețel), the auxiliary fort with a large vicus and amphitheatre, 4–6k; Germisara (Geoagiu-Băi) and Aquae (Călan), spa towns, 2–4k; Petrae, Blandiana and the Mureș stations 2–4k; the gold-mining vici of the Metaliferi at Ruda-Brad (the "Roman steps" galleries) and Băița 3–6k. Towns, forts and mines 31–50k.
2. Countryside: the Hațeg basin's villas and the Mureș and Strei villages at 6–10 per km² of lowland, the lawyered reading of Roman Dacia's 0.65–1.2M on ~100,000 km² for its richest basin; 39–80k. County 70–130k.
3. GDP multiplier. Maddison's Roman ratio (~1.2 × world) × role premium 1.5–2.0 relative to the Empire for the provincial capital with its council and procurator, the gold mines and the spas ("capital/entrepôt argued", with the Alburnus Maior wax tablets as the named fiscal source for the Dacian gold district, though Alburnus itself lies in Alba) → 1.8–2.5 (C12); the cap of 2.5 is not exceeded.

Denominator: 190M at 200 CE (McEvedy & Jones).

Result: population 0.037–0.068% (band 8–9); GDP 0.066–0.171% (band 9–10; 0.11% at the mid-range of 100k × 2.1; extension 11 at the very top). The second-highest documented-era GDP share in the gallery, below Constanța c. 200 (0.067–0.19%) and above Bucharest 2022 (0.122–0.128%).

- Killed: a county above ~150,000 (>21/km² on a half-mountain polygon); killed a multiplier above 2.5 (Ruling A would need the Dacian gold output quantified inside the polygon; the quantified tablets are Alba's).
- Killed: counting Apulum (Alba), Alburnus Maior (Alba) or Tibiscum (Caraș-Severin).

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 8.** Nameable command and chokepoint: the Dacian royal capital complex of the Orăștie Mountains (Sarmizegetusa Regia and its fortresses, World Heritage), the seat of Burebista's and Decebalus's kingdom; the Iron Gate of Transylvania pass and the battles of Tapae (88 and 101); the Roman provincial capital at Ulpia Traiana; the Metaliferi gold district; the Jiu valley, Romania's hard-coal basin, and the Hunedoara steelworks (1884), the country's largest to the 1960s. Not 9: the kingdom and the province were regional powers, and the coal and steel were national, not continental.
- **Mind 8.** Nameable claims: the element tellurium, first identified in 1782 in ore from Săcărâmb (Nagyág) by Müller von Reichenstein; the Turdaș signs (Torma, 1875–) in the Danubian proto-writing debate; the sanctuaries and "andesite sun" of Sarmizegetusa Regia; the Hațeg dwarf dinosaurs described by Franz Nopcsa (born at Săcel, 1877), a founding case of island dwarfism; Corvin Castle and the Densuș church built of Roman spolia; Aurel Vlaicu (born at Binținți, 1882) and his 1910 aircraft. A chemical element and a palaeontological paradigm carry the peg to 8.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** Band 10 holds only at the top of the Window 1 range; the mid-range is band 9. Census-grade band 8 is reached only in 1910.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 (~5×) and the Dacian c. 50 BCE figure (~3–4×); the c. 200 CE GDP figure is ~2.5–3×; the 1870–1910 figures are 1.2–1.6×.
- **Boundary smearing.** Pre-1948 figures take Hunyad county less the Cugir–Șibot corner (±10k, 3%); the 1930 figure likewise.
- **Double-count risk.** RO121 Alba (Apulum, Alburnus Maior and the Cugir corner; the Tărtăria tablets), RO422 Caraș-Severin (Tibiscum and the Bistra valley), RO421 Arad (Zam and the lower Mureș), RO412 Gorj (the Jiu's exit and the Parâng).
- **Citation flags.** The Hunyad county totals (257,459 in 1869; 303,838 in 1900; 340,100 in 1910) are from the census summaries, not the district tables (mek.oszk.hu is blocked); the Ulpia Traiana population (20–30k) is the literature's range; Strabo's and Lydus's figures are taken at face value under the ancient-source rule.
