# RO113 · Cluj County (Județul Cluj), Romania

**UNIT** RO113 (NUTS-3) = Cluj County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 6,674 km², seat Cluj-Napoca; municipalities Cluj-Napoca, Câmpia Turzii, Dej, Gherla, Turda; town Huedin; 75 communes. The Apuseni (Gilău, Vlădeasa) in the west (~25%), the Someș plateau and the Transylvanian Plain's western hills (~60%), the Arieș–Mureș corridor at Turda. Historic components: Kolozs County (5,149 km²; 286,687 in 1910) with Cluj, the Turda district of Torda-Aranyos County and the Dej–Gherla district of Szolnok-Doboka County; the interwar counties of Cluj, Turda (part) and Someș (part). 1950–1968 the territory was the core of the Cluj Region (polygon not used). Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: Iclod-group and Petrești settlement of the Someș plateau and the Transylvanian Plain.
**Documented-era peak** Window 4, c. 200 CE: Napoca, colonia and seat of the procurator of Dacia Porolissensis, with Potaissa and the legionary fortress of V Macedonica; band 9 at the lawyered top (range 9–10). Census-grade peak 1850–1910 (band 8).

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 6,674 km² (INS); the Apuseni quarter is excluded: usable plateau, plain and valley land 4,500–5,000 km².
2. Cultures at the moment: the Iclod group (type site Iclod on the Someșul Mic, inside the polygon) with its fortified settlements and cemeteries, Petrești painted ware and the first Tiszapolgár copper; Iclod, Zau de Câmpie's neighbours, Cluj-Napoca's own findspots and the Turda salt springs.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied. → 9,000–15,000.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: farming densities on the Apuseni; killed any county total above ~20,000 (>4/km² usable).
- Survives: 15,000 people means ~75–150 villages and hamlets of 100–200 across the Someș, Nadăș, Arieș and Fizeș valleys at 5–8 km spacing, consistent with the Iclod-group site density; cropland need under 3% of the usable area.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Deep score — Window 4, c. 200 CE (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Potaissa (Turda): the legionary fortress of V Macedonica from 168, 23.4 ha, a medium-sized legionary camp with its 5,000–6,000 men; the city a municipium Septimium with ius Italicum, "at its height housing as many as 20,000 people" in the standard reference summaries; the Turda salt mines worked under the Romans. → 15–25k with the legion and canabae.
2. Napoca (Cluj-Napoca): municipium under Hadrian, colonia under Marcus Aurelius, seat of the procurator of Dacia Porolissensis in the third century, the provincial capital in all but name; walled area of the order of 30 ha with suburbs → 10–15k.
3. Rural: the Someș plateau and Transylvanian Plain villae, the Gilău and Cășeiu forts' vici, the salt roads, at 6–10/km² across the usable county → 35–60k.
→ 60–100k.
4. GDP multiplier per C12: provincial-capital premium 1.6–2.0 (the rubric's "imperial capital" band, argued for the procurator's seat plus one of Dacia's two legions) × the Roman Empire's per-capita ratio to the world 1.0–1.2 → 1.6–2.4.

Denominator: 190M at 200 CE (rubric floors table and McEvedy & Jones).

Result: population 0.0316–0.0526% (band 8–9); GDP 0.0505–0.126% (band 9–10; 10 at and above 0.09%, i.e. the upper third of the range).

Veto notes
- Killed: counting the legion twice (in the fortress and in Potaissa's 20,000); the 15–25k figure holds both.
- Killed: applying the premium to the whole of Dacia Porolissensis; only the county's own population is counted, the premium standing for the procurator's court, the legion's pay and the salt administration concentrated in it.
- Consensus: Potaissa 8–12k with the legion, Napoca 5–8k, county 30–45k, multiplier ~1.1–1.2 → 0.018–0.029%.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is the Window 1 floor artefact. The documented-era record (c. 200 CE) rests on the standard summaries' 20,000 for Potaissa, an order-of-magnitude estimate for Napoca and a density allocation for the countryside, plus the provincial-capital premium; band 10 holds only in the upper third of the range. The census-grade peak (1850–1910, band 8) is the uncontested alternative.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 (~4–5×) and the c. 200 CE GDP figure (~3–4×).
- **Boundary smearing.** Three pre-1918 counties contribute; pre-1956 figures are reconstructions (±15k).
- **Double-count risk.** RO116 Sălaj (Porolissum, the province's nominal capital), RO121 Alba (Apulum and the Dacian provincial command, not to be double-counted with Napoca's procurator), RO125 Mureș and RO112 Bistrița-Năsăud (the Transylvanian Plain), RO121 Alba again for the Torda-Aranyos remainder.
- **Citation flags.** No read walled-area figure for Napoca; the 2022 GDP uses the 5.2% share from the INS county accounts summary.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1 (and the upper third of the c. 200 CE range); band 8 GDP held c. 1850–1910; band 7 population held 1850–1977.
