# RO213 · Iași County (Județul Iași), Romania

**UNIT** RO213 (NUTS-3) = Iași County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 5,476 km², seat Iași; municipalities Iași and Pașcani; towns Hârlău, Podu Iloaiei, Târgu Frumos; 93 communes. The Moldavian Plain of the Jijia and Bahlui, the Iași hills and the Prut and Siret margins, all habitable hill country without mountains. Historic components: the interwar Iași County (seat Iași) with the Pașcani area of Baia County and the Hârlău area of Botoșani County, less the Fălciu–Vaslui margin. 1950–1968 the territory was the core of the Iași Region (polygon not used). Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: Cucuteni A settlement of the Moldavian Plain, with the culture's type site (Cucuteni-Cetățuie) and Hăbășești inside the polygon.
**Documented-era peak** Windows 10–11, c. 1650–1850: Iași as the capital of Moldavia (1564–1859); band 8 at the lawyered top (range 7–8), tied by 1912. Population band 7 from 1850 to 1977.

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 5,476 km² (INS), all plain and hill country; usable area taken as the whole polygon.
2. Cultures at the moment: Cucuteni A, the classic phase of the Cucuteni–Trypillia complex, in its heartland: Cucuteni-Cetățuie (the type site, excavated by Schmidt in 1909–10), Hăbășești (the fully excavated Cucuteni A village of 44 houses in two rings, Dumitrescu 1949–50), Ruginoasa, Scânteia, Bălțați and the hundreds of Cucuteni findspots of the county repertory (Monah & Cucoș's national repertory lists some 1,800 Cucuteni sites, with the Iași–Botoșani plain the densest zone).
3. Density 2.2–3.7 persons/km², the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities raised to the top of its published range for the Cucuteni core: a quarter of the county's recorded Cucuteni findspots occupied at once (~100 sites) at Hăbășești's 150–300 people each gives 15–30k, so 12–20k is adopted as the lawyered range. Equivalence rule applied.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian; Cucuteni copper and painted-ware exchange attested but unquantified).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: the Trypillia giant-settlement densities of the Ukrainian forest-steppe (4100–3600 BCE), not attested in Moldavia, and any county total above ~27,000 (>5/km²).
- Survives: 20,000 people means ~100 villages of 200 at 5–7 km spacing across the Bahlui, Jijia, Miletin and Prut valleys, within the recorded site density; cropland need under 4% of the polygon.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Deep score — Windows 10–11, c. 1650–1850, the capital of Moldavia (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. c. 1650: Iași, capital since 1564, under Vasile Lupu (the Three Hierarchs 1639, the Vasilian Academy 1640, the first Moldavian press 1642); Bandini's visitation of 1646–47 and Paul of Aleppo's diary of 1653–56 describe a large, cosmopolitan town; city 15–25k at the top of the published readings, the Iași district's villages 50–80k → 65–105k. c. 1700 (Cantemir's court, Peter the Great's visit of 1711): 70–110k.
2. 1850: Iași 60–65k (65,745 at the 1859 census), one of the largest cities of south-eastern Europe outside Istanbul; the district's villages 140–170k → 200–235k (Moldavia's 1859 census for the Iași district scaled to the polygon).
3. GDP multiplier per C12: capital premium 1.6–2.0 (the rubric's "imperial capital" band, argued for a principality's seat with the court, the Metropolis, the boyar households and the Phanariote administration) × Eastern Europe's per-capita ratio to the world 0.9–1.0 → 1.44–2.0 c. 1650–1700; for 1850, Moldavia 0.9–1.1 × county index 1.4–1.8 (the city a third of the county) → 1.26–2.0.

Denominators: 470M at 1650 (Willcox 1931, C2 deep floor, citation flag open); 610M at 1700; 1,091M at 1850 (Willcox 1931).

Result: c. 1650, population 0.0138–0.0223% (band 6–7), GDP 0.020–0.045% (band 7–8); c. 1700, 0.0115–0.018% and 0.0165–0.036% (band 7–8); 1850, population 0.0183–0.0215% (band 7), GDP 0.023–0.043% (band 7–8). Band 8 holds in the upper part of each range.

Veto notes
- Killed: seventeenth-century travellers' figures of 40,000 and more for Iași, which the house counts of the 1640s do not support; the city is bounded at 25k before 1800.
- Killed: crediting the Phanariote court's tribute flows or Moldavia's whole grain export to the capital (double-count rule); only the court, administration and market functions concentrated in the county are credited, which is what the premium stands for.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 8.** Nameable command: the capital of Moldavia for three centuries (1564–1859), co-capital of the United Principalities (1859–62) and the capital of Romania in 1916–18, when government, king and general staff sat in the city. A principality's and then a kingdom's wartime seat, so 8.
- **Mind 8.** Nameable institutions: the type site of the Cucuteni culture; the first Romanian university (1860) and the Junimea society (1863), the cradle of modern Romanian letters (Eminescu, Creangă, Maiorescu); the Vasilian Academy (1640) and the first Moldavian press (1642); the Metropolis of Moldavia. National rank with a European-rank archaeological eponym, so 8.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is the Window 1 floor artefact, here with a density pushed to the top of the Cucuteni core's published range. The documented-era record is spread over three near-level moments (c. 1650, 1850, 1912); each reaches band 8 only in the upper part of its range.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 (~4×); the c. 1650 figure is ~2–3×.
- **Boundary smearing.** Pre-1948 figures are reconstructions; the Pașcani and Hârlău transfers are ±15k.
- **Double-count risk.** RO212 Botoșani (the Cucuteni heartland continues north, Trușești), RO216 Vaslui (the Fălciu margin), RO214 Neamț and RO215 Suceava (the Pașcani–Baia area and Suceava's earlier capital role, not to be double-counted with Iași's).
- **Citation flags.** No read figure for seventeenth-century Iași; the 2022 GDP is interpolated between the 2019 and 2023 INS values.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1; band 7–8 GDP held c. 1640–1930; band 7 population held 1850–1977.
