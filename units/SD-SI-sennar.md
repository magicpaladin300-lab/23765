# SD-SI · Sennar State (Wilayat Sinnar), Sudan

**UNIT** SD-SI (ISO 3166-2) = Sennar State. Scale flag (C14): an ADM1 unit of 37,844 km², five to eight times a NUTS-3 county; every share below carries the polygon-size caveat.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (state of 1994): 37,844 km², seat Singa; localities Sennar, Singa, East Sennar, Abu Hugar, Ad-Dindir, Ad-Dali and Al-Mazmum, As-Suki. The southern Gezira between the Blue Nile and the Dinder–Rahad plain, with Sennar town and dam, Jebel Moya and the Dinder National Park. Historic components: the heartland of the Funj sultanate of Sennar (1504–1821) with its capital, the Turco-Egyptian Sennar mudiriya, the condominium's Blue Nile (Fung) Province, Blue Nile Province of 1956–74 and Central Region (1974–94). Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: the African Humid Period savanna and the Blue Nile valley at the equivalence-rule densities.
**Documented-era peak** Window 10, c. 1700: Sennar, capital of the Funj sultanate, put at 100,000 inhabitants by Charles-Jacques Poncet (1699), with the southern Gezira as the sultanate's granary and the Fazughli gold and Red Sea trade passing through the court; band 10 at the lawyered top (range 8–10). Census-grade peak: population band 7 (2018), 8 at the top of the 2024 trend.

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 37,844 km²; the Blue Nile strip and its terraces (~4,000 km²) and the clay plain of the southern Gezira and the Dinder (~34,000 km²), savanna in the African Humid Period.
2. Communities at the moment: the Khartoum Neolithic tradition up the Blue Nile (Shaheinab-type herder-fishers; Rabak and the White Nile sites lie to the west) and savanna herders; the polygon's early sites are few, so the equivalence rule for a non-literate society applies at the high end.
3. Density, equivalence rule, high end: 0.5–1.0 persons/km² on the river strip, 0.15–0.4 persons/km² on the savanna (Hassan's Saharan–Sahelian Neolithic range, C14). → 7,100–17,600, reported 7.1–17.6k.
4. GDP multiplier 0.8–1.0 (pastoral-agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: European farmed-zone densities anywhere; killed a total above ~21,000 (>0.55/km² over the whole state).
- Survives: 17,600 people means ~90–175 camps and hamlets of 100 along the river and the Dinder; grazing and cropland need under 5% of the polygon.
- Allocation flag: no excavated Chalcolithic site in the polygon anchors the figure.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across the humid-period Sahara and Sahel gives 1–2 million people against a 5M world floor, a fifth to two fifths of humanity; with Europe's farmed zone at the same stacking the two regions exceed the floor.

## Deep score — Window 10, c. 1700 (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Sennar, the Funj capital founded by Amara Dunqas in 1504, at its height after Badi II Abu Daqn (1644–81) had taken Taqali and Fazughli: the French physician Charles-Jacques Poncet, who stayed there in 1699, put the town at 100,000 inhabitants (Theodor Krump in 1701 described it as the greatest trading town of the region); the early-source rule carries Poncet's figure at the top of range, 50–100k, though Schuver (1881) and modern scholars doubt that it ever exceeded 20–30k. The hinterland: the southern Gezira's rain-fed sorghum villages and the Blue Nile riverain, the sultanate's granary, 130–350k (a quarter to a third of a Funj core lawyered at 0.8–1.2M). County 180–450k.
2. GDP multiplier 0.9–1.26: Maddison's Africa ratio (~0.7 of world c. 1700) × role premium 1.3–1.8 relative to the region for the capital of the largest state of the Nilotic Sudan, the terminus of the Fazughli gold, the Suakin and Egyptian caravan trade and the royal monopolies that Poncet and Krump describe ("capital/entrepôt argued"; no quantified ledger survives, so the 2.0–2.5 band is not claimed).

Denominator: 610M at 1700 (McEvedy & Jones); on the Willcox-interpolated 582M the shares rise by 5%, and carried back to Badi II's apogee c. 1650 on Willcox's 470M the top would reach 0.113%.

Result: population 0.030–0.074% (band 8–9); GDP 0.027–0.093% (band 8–10; 0.052% at the mid-range of 300k × 1.06; 10 at the lawyered top, where Poncet's 100,000 and the full hinterland and premium stack).

Veto notes
- Killed: a multiplier above 1.26 (no fiscal ledger); killed a hinterland above 350k (the whole Funj core cannot have exceeded ~1.2M, and Gezira and the White Nile take the rest).
- Killed: counting Fazughli's gold at source (SD-NB) or the Gezira north of the state line.
- Early-source flag: Poncet's 100,000 is the one contemporary count; it is "innocent until proven guilty" under the rubric and is contradicted by Schuver's 1881 inference and the site's extent. Consensus halves the city and the hinterland.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 7.** Nameable command: Sennar, capital of the Funj sultanate for three centuries (1504–1821), the largest Muslim state of the Nilotic Sudan; the Sennar Dam (1925), which waters the Gezira Scheme, the largest irrigation project under a single management in the world; Sennar Junction on the Khartoum–El Obeid and Kassala lines; the Dinder gate toward Ethiopia. Not 8: the sultanate commanded no chokepoint beyond the middle Nile.
- **Mind 6.** Nameable claims: the Singa skull (1924), an archaic Homo sapiens calvaria of c. 130,000 years, one of Africa's key fossils; Jebel Moya, the largest excavated cemetery in sub-Saharan Africa; the Funj Chronicle (Katib al-Shuna) and the court's Islamic learning under the Funj. Regional institutions and two reference finds, so 6.

## Flags
- **Scale (C14).** An ADM1 state of 37,844 km²; at NUTS-3 scale its shares would be a fifth to an eighth. Every band above is a state-level band.
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is a Window 1 density allocation. The documented-era record is band 10 only at the top of range, where Poncet's 100,000, a hinterland of 350k and the full capital premium stack; the mid-range is band 9 and the consensus band 7–8. The census-grade series is band 7, touching 8 only on the 2024 trend.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 (~7–8×); the c. 1700 GDP figure (~3.5×) on the early-source rule. The contemporary figures are 1.0–1.1×.
- **Boundary smearing.** Pre-1994 figures are allocations from Blue Nile Province and Central Region; the Funj hinterland is a judgement share of a core without counts.
- **Double-count risk.** SD-GZ Gezira (the Gezira Scheme's main blocks and the Funj core north of the line), SD-NB Blue Nile (Fazughli's gold at source), SD-NW White Nile (Alodia's and the Funj's western Gezira), SD-KH Khartoum (Soba, Alodia's capital).
- **Citation flags.** Poncet's 100,000 is a traveller's count disputed since 1881; no Funj population total was retrieved; the 2024 population and the GDP shares are estimates.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 in Window 1 and, at the top of range only, c. 1650–1720; band 8–9 held c. 1550–1760 on the lawyered sultanate figures; band 7 on population since the 1980s.
