# RO111 · Bihor County (Județul Bihor), Romania

**UNIT** RO111 (NUTS-3) = Bihor County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 7,544 km², seat Oradea; municipalities Oradea, Beiuș, Marghita, Salonta; towns Aleșd, Nucet, Săcueni, Ștei, Valea lui Mihai, Vașcău; 91 communes. The Crișana plain of the Crișul Repede and Crișul Negru (~45%), the Western Hills (~25%) and the Apuseni (Bihor, Pădurea Craiului, Codru-Moma, ~30%). Historic components: the Romanian part (roughly 70%) of Bihar County (10,609 km²; 646,301 in 1910), i.e. all but its Debrecen–Berettyó districts now in Hungary's Hajdú-Bihar, plus the Valea lui Mihai area of Szatmár; the interwar Bihor County. 1950–1968 the territory was the Oradea, then Crișana, region (polygons not used). The 1940–44 Vienna Award line cut the county. Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: Tiszapolgár and Salca-group settlement of the Crișana plain and hills.
**Documented-era peak** Window 11, 1850 (Willcox floor), band 8 (9 only at the extreme GDP top), tied by 1910 (band 8). Medieval Oradea (c. 1450) and the Ottoman eyalet of Varat (c. 1660) reach band 7–8 at the top.
**WINNING PATHWAY** GDP; population band 8 at 1850–1910.

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 7,544 km² (INS); the Apuseni third is excluded: usable plain and hills 5,200–5,400 km².
2. Cultures at the moment: Tiszapolgár and its Salca group (type site Oradea-Salca inside the polygon), the Early Copper Age of the Crișana plain following the Herpály–Tisza tells; the Suplacu de Barcău cluster and the Crișul Negru terraces.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied. → 10,400–16,200, reported 10–16k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: farming densities on the Apuseni; killed any county total above ~22,000 (>4/km² usable).
- Survives: 16,000 people means ~80–160 hamlets of 100–200 at 5–8 km spacing across the Barcău, Crișul Repede, Crișul Negru and Ier valleys; cropland need under 3% of the usable area.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Deep score — Window 11, 1850 (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Bihar County held some 440–470k people at the 1850 Austrian census and 555k in 1869; the polygon's share (roughly 72–75% by area and population) gives 330–360k, with Oradea at about 20k.
2. GDP per head: Hungary against the world 1.1–1.3 (Maddison: Hungary $1,092 vs world $873 in 1870) × county index 1.0–1.2 (Oradea a leading regional city with its bishoprics, the Crișana's grain and wine, the Beiuș and Vașcău iron and the Apuseni timber) → 1.1–1.56× world.

Denominator: 1,091M (Willcox 1931, the lowest published 1850 total; C2 deep floor, citation flag open).

Result: population 0.0302–0.0330% (band 8); GDP 0.0333–0.0515% (band 8; 9 only at the extreme top).

Tie, 1910: 485–500k (Bihar 646,301 less the Hungarian districts, plus Valea lui Mihai) over 1,739M → 0.0279–0.0288% (band 8); GDP ×1.37–1.71 (Hungary 1.37 × county 1.0–1.25, Oradea 64,169 and the seventh city of Hungary) → 0.038–0.049% (band 8).

Veto notes
- Killed: counting the Debrecen districts of Bihar County, which lie in Hungary.
- Consensus: the UN's 1,260M for 1850 and a county index of 1.0 give 0.027% and 0.030%.

## Earlier candidates (medieval Oradea and the eyalet)
- **Window 7, c. 1240.** Bihar, one of the kingdom's largest castle counties; the bishopric of Oradea (moved from Biharia c. 1092) with the shrine of St Ladislaus (canonised 1192), the kingdom's chief pilgrimage site and ordeal court (the *Regestrum Varadinense*, 389 cases of 1208–35, a unique judicial register). 32–50k over 360M with a regional-centre premium (1.2–1.6 × 0.9) → 0.0096–0.020% GDP, band 6–7.
- **Window 8, c. 1450.** Oradea at its medieval height: royal necropolis (Ladislaus I, Andrew II, Ladislaus IV, Queen Mary, Sigismund of Luxembourg in 1437), János Vitéz's humanist court and observatory (1455), the cathedral; city 8–15k at the top of the published claims (popular summaries say 15–20k), county 40–60k over 350M; premium 1.6–2.0 (the rubric's "imperial capital" band argued for the kingdom's second ecclesiastical centre and its richest bishopric) × 0.88–1.0 → 0.016–0.034% GDP, band 7–8 at the top. Not the record: the city figure is a popular claim and the premium is argued.
- **Window 9, 1552.** The dica (gate-tax) register of Bihar County, a named county-wide fiscal source: 70–85k in the polygon over 487M with the regional premium (1.06–1.6) → 0.015–0.028% GDP, band 7–8 at the top edge. The 2.0–2.5 "quantified fiscal documentation" band is not applied: a tax register documents the population, not a commercial premium.
- **Window 10, c. 1660.** Oradea as the capital of the Ottoman eyalet of Varat (1660–92) after the siege of 1660: 50–80k over 498M with the eyalet-capital premium (1.44–1.8) → 0.0145–0.029% GDP, band 6–8 at the top.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 7.** Nameable command: Oradea's fortress, among the great strongholds of the Habsburg–Ottoman frontier (sieges of 1598, 1660 and 1692), the capital of the eyalet of Varat (1660–92), and the seat of one of medieval Hungary's largest castle counties; the Bihor passes (Crișul Repede) into Transylvania. A frontier fortress and provincial seat, so 7.
- **Mind 8.** Nameable institutions of European rank: the shrine and royal necropolis of St Ladislaus at Oradea, the Hungarian kingdom's chief pilgrimage site; the *Regestrum Varadinense* (1208–35), the only surviving register of ordeals by hot iron; Vitéz's observatory (1455) and the Oradea meridian of Regiomontanus's and Peuerbach's tables (*Tabulae Varadienses*), the reference meridian of fifteenth-century astronomical tables; the Catholic and Greek-Catholic bishoprics and the Art Nouveau city of 1900. Ady Endre and Arany János fall under the event-fame ban as residents or births.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is the Window 1 floor artefact. The documented-era record is 1850 on Willcox's floor, tied by 1910 within rounding; band 9 appears only at the extreme GDP top of 1850. The medieval Oradea moments (c. 1450, 1552, 1660) reach band 8 only at the top of argued-premium ranges.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 only (~4–5×).
- **Boundary smearing.** The polygon is 70–75% of Bihar County; the 1850 and 1910 shares are apportionments (±20k).
- **Double-count risk.** Hungary's Hajdú-Bihar (the Debrecen districts of Bihar, not on the list), RO115 Satu Mare (Valea lui Mihai's origin), RO421 Arad and RO116 Sălaj (the Apuseni and Barcău margins), RO113 Cluj (the Crișul Repede pass).
- **Citation flags.** Oradea's medieval population (15–20k) is a popular claim; the 1552 dica totals and the 2022 GDP are inferred.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1; band 8 held c. 1850–1910; band 7 held 1930–1977.
