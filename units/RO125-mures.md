# RO125 · Mureș County (Județul Mureș), Romania

**UNIT** RO125 (NUTS-3) = Mureș County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 6,714 km², seat Târgu Mureș; municipalities Târgu Mureș, Reghin, Sighișoara, Târnăveni; towns Iernut, Luduș, Miercurea Nirajului, Sângeorgiu de Pădure, Sărmașu, Sovata, Ungheni; 91 communes. The middle Mureș valley from Deda to Luduș, the Niraj and Târnava valleys, the eastern Transylvanian Plain and the Gurghiu–Călimani volcanic range. Historic components: most of Hungarian Maros-Torda (219,589 in 1910) less the Toplița corner, the Târnăveni two thirds of Kis-Küküllő (116,091), the Sighișoara district of Nagy-Küküllő, and the Luduș–Sărmașu corner of Torda-Aranyos and Kolozs; the interwar counties of Mureș and Târnava-Mică (part), Târnava-Mare (part) and Turda (part). Pre-1948 figures are allocations to the current territory; the INS current-territory series begins in 1948. Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: Petrești and Tiszapolgár settlement of the Mureș and Târnava valleys and the Transylvanian Plain.
**Documented-era peak** Window 12, 1900–1910: Târgu Mureș (the Székely seat, the Transylvanian Table of 1754, the Palace of Culture of 1913) and Sighișoara at the head of a county of 410–440k; band 8 (GDP plateau 1850–1910). Population peak 1787–1850, band 8 at the top of range.

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 6,714 km² (INS); the Gurghiu and Călimani volcanic range (~1,200 km²) is excluded: usable valleys, plateau and plain 5,000–5,500 km².
2. Cultures at the moment: Petrești on the Mureș and Târnave (Iernut, Sighișoara), Tiszapolgár and the Iclod group on the Transylvanian Plain, with Cucuteni–Ariușd contacts up the Mureș; the Luduș and Târgu Mureș terraces are among the densest Chalcolithic site lists in Transylvania.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied. → 10,000–16,500, reported 10–16.5k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: farming densities on the Gurghiu and Călimani; killed any county total above ~22,000 (>4/km² usable).
- Survives: 16,500 people means ~80–165 villages and hamlets of 100–200 along the Mureș, Niraj, Târnave and the plain's valleys at 4–7 km spacing; cropland need under 3% of the usable area.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 6.** Nameable but regional: Târgu Mureș as the Székely seat and the venue of some forty Transylvanian Diets (1540s–1707); the Transylvanian Table (1754–1861), the principality's court of appeal; Sighișoara's citadel on the Târnava road; Azomureș (1962), Romania's largest fertilizer plant; the Sărmășel gas field (1909), the first in the Transylvanian basin. No continental chokepoint.
- **Mind 8.** Nameable claim of world scale: the Bolyais at Târgu Mureș, Farkas teaching at the Reformed College (1804–51) and János, inventor of non-Euclidean geometry (the Appendix of 1832), living and dying there (1833–60), with their manuscripts in the Teleki–Bolyai Library (1802), one of the first public libraries of the Hungarian kingdom; Sighișoara's medieval citadel (World Heritage); the Wietenberg type site; Sándor Petőfi's last journey from Târgu Mureș (1849). One foundational mathematician and a library carry the peg to 8.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is the Window 1 floor artefact. The documented-era record is a long plateau: GDP band 8 from 1850 to 1910 (and band 7–8 back to 1450 at the top of range), with 1910 the highest top (0.043%); population band 8 only at the top of range in 1787 and 1850.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 only (~4–5×); the 1787–1910 figures are 1.1–1.4×.
- **Boundary smearing.** Pre-1948 figures rebuild the polygon from five Hungarian counties and four interwar counties, anchored on 1930 and 1948 (±20k, 4–5%); the Hungarian district tables could not be fetched (mek.oszk.hu is blocked).
- **Double-count risk.** RO124 Harghita (Toplița, Sovata's Székely hinterland, the Niraj headwaters), RO121 Alba (Blaj and the lower Târnava Mică), RO126 Sibiu (Mediaș and the Târnava Mare below Sighișoara), RO113 Cluj (Sărmașu's plain), RO122 Brașov (the Târnava Mare above Sighișoara).
- **Citation flags.** The 1787–1910 numerators are reconstructions from the county totals (Maros-Torda 165,627 in 1869, 197,618 in 1900, 219,589 in 1910; Kis-Küküllő 100,161, 109,197, 116,091); Kubinyi's 1495 rows were not fetched.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1; band 8 held c. 1850–1914 on GDP; band 7 on population c. 1495–1948.
