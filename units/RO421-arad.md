# RO421 · Arad County (Județul Arad), Romania

**UNIT** RO421 (NUTS-3) = Arad County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 7,754 km², seat Arad; towns Chișineu-Criș, Curtici, Ineu, Lipova, Nădlac, Pâncota, Pecica, Sântana, Sebiș; 68 communes. The Arad plain and the Crișul Alb plain in the west, the Mureș corridor from Nădlac to Săvârșin and Petriș, the Zarand, Highiș and Codru-Moma mountains and the Hălmagiu basin in the east. Historic components: the Hungarian county of Arad (6,443 km²) less its strip now in Hungary (Elek and its villages), plus the western half of old Zaránd (merged into Arad in 1876), the Lipova district of Temes, and Nădlac from Csanád. Pre-1948 figures are allocations to the current territory; the INS current-territory series begins in 1948. Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: Tisza–Herpály and Tiszapolgár settlement of the Arad and Crișul Alb plains and the Mureș terraces.
**Documented-era peak** Window 12, 1900: Arad city (56,260 in 1900; 63,166 in 1910, the kingdom's sixth to eighth city) with the Weitzer wagon works, the mills and distilleries and the Arad–Csanád railway company at the head of a county of 435–460k; band 8 (9 at the very top). Population peak 1850, band 8.

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 7,754 km² (INS); the Zarand, Highiș and Codru-Moma mountains (~1,800 km²) and the Crișul Alb and Mureș marshes (~500 km²) are excluded: usable plain, terraces and the Hălmagiu basin 5,000–5,800 km².
2. Cultures at the moment: late Tisza and Herpály groups passing into Tiszapolgár on the Arad plain (the Mureș–Crișul Alb interfluve is the eastern edge of the Alföld tell zone), with Vinča-influenced sites along the Mureș (Pecica, Semlac) and the Zarand foothills.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied. → 10,000–17,400, reported 10–17.4k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: farming densities on the Zarand and Codru-Moma; killed any county total above ~23,000 (>4/km² usable).
- Survives: 17,400 people means ~90–170 villages and hamlets of 100–200 along the Mureș, the Crișul Alb, the Ier and the plain's watercourses at 4–7 km spacing; cropland need under 3% of the usable area.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 7.** Nameable command: the Arad fortress (1763–83), one of the largest Vauban-type works of the monarchy, the last seat of the Hungarian revolutionary government (August 1849) and the place of the thirteen martyrs; the Mureș salt road and the Lipova chamber; Transylvania's first railway (Arad–Alba Iulia, 1868) and the Arad–Csanád company; the Curtici frontier crossing, Romania's main western rail gate; the Astra wagon works. No continental chokepoint, so 7.
- **Mind 6.** Nameable but regional: the Preparandia of 1812, the first Romanian teacher-training school; the Arad theatre (1817) and conservatory (1833), among the earliest in Hungary; the Palace of Culture (1913); Ioan Slavici, Moise Nicoară and the Arad Romanian national movement; Pecica's Bronze Age tell as a type site. Nothing of world scale, so 6.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is the Window 1 floor artefact. The documented-era record is a plateau: GDP band 8 from 1850 to 1910 with the 1900 top touching band 9; population band 8 in 1850 and at the top of range in 1900. Choosing 1850 or 1870 over 1900 changes the band only at the top.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 only (~4–5×); the 1850–1910 figures are 1.1–1.5× consensus.
- **Boundary smearing.** Pre-1948 figures rebuild the polygon from Hungarian Arad county (less Elek), old Zaránd's western half, the Lipova district and Nădlac (±15–25k, 3–5%); the 1930 figure adds the Lipova district to the Romanian county of 423,469.
- **Double-count risk.** RO424 Timiș (the Lipova district's Temes past, the Mureș's south bank), RO111 Bihor (old Zaránd's east and the Crișul Alb headwaters), RO423 Hunedoara (Zam and the upper Mureș), Hungarian Békés and Csongrád-Csanád (Elek, Battonya, the Csanád villages).
- **Citation flags.** The Hungarian census county totals for 1850–1910 and Kubinyi's 1495 Arad and Zaránd rows could not be fetched (mek.oszk.hu and tti.abtk.hu are blocked); the pre-1948 numerators are reconstructions from the city figures (Arad 32,725 in 1869, 56,260 in 1900, 63,166 in 1910) and the 1930 Romanian county, and are flagged for verification.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1; band 8 held c. 1850–1910 on GDP; band 7 on population 1787–1948.
