# RO223 · Constanța County (Județul Constanța), Romania

**UNIT** RO223 (NUTS-3) = Constanța County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 7,071 km², seat Constanța; municipalities Constanța, Mangalia, Medgidia; towns Băneasa, Cernavodă, Eforie, Hârșova, Murfatlar, Năvodari, Negru Vodă, Ovidiu, Techirghiol; 58 communes. Southern Dobruja between the Danube (Hârșova–Cernavodă–Ostrov) and the Black Sea coast from Corbu to Vama Veche, with the Sinoe–Istria lagoons in the north-east and the Carasu valley (today the Danube–Black Sea Canal) across the middle. Historic components: the Ottoman kazas of Köstence, Mangalia, Hârșova and Medgidia; the Romanian county of Constanța from 1878, plus the Ostrov–Băneasa strip that belonged to Durostor county (1913–40). Pre-1948 figures are allocations to the current territory. Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: the Hamangia culture (type site Baia-Hamangia; the Cernavodă necropolis) passing into Gumelnița on the coast, the lake shores, the Carasu valley and the Danube bank.
**Documented-era peak** Window 4, c. 200 CE: Tomis as metropolis of the Pontic cities and chief port of Moesia Inferior, with Histria, Callatis, Tropaeum Traiani, Axiopolis, Capidava and Carsium and their epigraphically attested vici; band 10 at the lawyered top (range 9–10; open-top extension 11 at the very top). Window 5, c. 400 (Tomis as capital of the province of Scythia), ties within range. Census-grade peak: GDP band 7 (1912, 1977, 2022–2023 PPP), population band 6 (1977).

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 7,071 km² (INS); the lagoons and lakes (~500 km²) and the dry central and southern plateau (Medgidia–Cobadin–Negru Vodă, under 450 mm of rain, without Chalcolithic settlement) are excluded: usable coast, lake shores, Carasu valley, Casimcea valley and Danube bank 3,500–4,500 km².
2. Cultures at the moment: late Hamangia (Baia-Hamangia, the Cernavodă necropolis with over 400 graves, Medgidia, Cheia, Târgușor) passing into Gumelnița A on the Danube tells (Hârșova, Carcaliu) and the lake shores; the Varna horizon is 150 km down the coast. Dobruja is the richest Chalcolithic cemetery landscape in Romania.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009), as for Călărași, Giurgiu and Ialomița. Equivalence rule applied. → 7,000–13,500, reported 7–13.5k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: farming densities on the dry plateau; killed any county total above ~18,000 (>4/km² usable).
- Survives: 13,500 people means ~70–130 villages and hamlets of 100–200 along the coast, the lakes, the Carasu and the Danube at 4–7 km spacing, which the Hamangia site lists support; cropland need under 3% of the usable area.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Deep score — Window 4, c. 200 CE (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Cities, built from walled areas at 200–300 per hectare with suburbs: Tomis, metropolis of the Pontic koinon (the Hexapolis) and the province's chief port, 20–30k; Callatis (Mangalia) 8–12k; Histria 6–10k; Tropaeum Traiani (Adamclisi), municipium from 170 and the largest Roman foundation of the region, 4–8k; Axiopolis (Cernavodă), Capidava, Carsium (Hârșova), Ulmetum (Pantelimon), Sacidava and the Danube forts 8–12k together. Towns 46–72k.
2. Countryside: some forty vici are named in the inscriptions of Dobruja (vicus Quintionis, vicus Secundini, vicus Celeris, vicus Clementianensis and the rest), most inside this polygon in the territories of Histria, Tomis, Callatis and Tropaeum; 150–250 villages at 150–300 people → 34–78k. County 80–150k; the lawyered top is the Roman imperial average density of ~20/km² applied to the most urbanized stretch of the lower Danube.
3. GDP multiplier. Maddison's Roman ratio (1 CE: Roman Empire ≈1.2 × world) × role premium 1.3–2.0 relative to the Empire for the metropolis, the port of the grain and fish trade and the three Greek cities ("capital/entrepôt argued", with the Horothesia of Laberius Maximus (100 CE) as the named fiscal document for Histria's fishing and customs revenues) → 1.6–2.4, as for Napoca and Romula (C12).

Denominator: 190M at 200 CE (McEvedy & Jones).

Result: population 0.042–0.079% (band 8–9); GDP 0.067–0.189% (band 9–10; 0.121% at the mid-range of 115k × 2.0; extension 11 at the very top). The highest documented-era GDP share in the gallery, above Bucharest 2022 (0.122–0.128%) and Napoca c. 200 (0.051–0.126%).

Veto notes
- Killed: a Ruling A piercing above 2.4×; Tomis's harbour dues and the Danube portorium are a few tenths of the district's agrarian product.
- Killed: counting Troesmis, Noviodunum, Aegyssus and Halmyris (Tulcea) or Durostorum (Silistra) in the numerator.
- Killed: a county above ~170,000 (>24/km², Roman Italy's level, on a semi-arid steppe).
- Window 5 tie: c. 400, Tomis as capital of Diocletian's province of Scythia with eight of its fifteen cities inside the polygon, 80–140k × 1.5–2.3 → 0.063–0.169% GDP (band 9–10); c. 550 under Justinian's rebuilding, 60–110k × 1.3–2.0 → 0.042–0.119% (band 8–10). The record is placed at the Severan peak of coin circulation (last quarter of the 2nd century to the 230s).

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 8.** Nameable command and chokepoint: Constanța, the largest port on the Black Sea and Romania's maritime gateway; the Danube–Black Sea Canal (1984), which shortens the river route by ~400 km; the Cernavodă nuclear plant (1996, 2007), the country's only one; the Mihail Kogălniceanu air base, NATO's principal Black Sea hub; Tomis as metropolis of the Pontic cities and capital of Scythia; the Saligny bridge of 1895, then the longest in Europe. Not 9: no continental monopoly comparable to Ploiești's oil.
- **Mind 8.** Nameable claims: Ovid's exile at Tomis (8–17 CE) and the Tristia and Epistulae ex Ponto written there; the Thinker of Hamangia from Cernavodă (c. 5000 BCE); Histria, the first Greek city of the western Pontus (657 BCE); the Tropaeum Traiani monument (109 CE); the Tomis mosaic edifice; the archbishopric of Tomis and the "Scythian monks" of the province, among them Dionysius Exiguus, deviser of the Anno Domini era, and John Cassian, both born in Scythia Minor c. 360–470 (a 9 is arguable on these two; the birthplace attribution is to the province, not the polygon, so 8). The Mamaia resort and Ceaușescu-era Neptun are event and leisure fame.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is the Window 1 floor artefact. The documented-era record (c. 200 CE) reaches band 10 only at the top of the range (150k people and the 2.4× multiplier together); its mid-range is band 9 (0.121%), and Window 5 c. 400 ties within range. Census-grade band 7 is held on GDP in 1912, 1977 and 2022–2023 (PPP).
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 (~4–5×) and the c. 200 CE GDP figure (~4×: consensus 50–70k people at 1.2–1.5×). The census-grade figures are 1–2×.
- **Pairing flag.** The 2023 PPP figure (0.0200%) carries the 2023 county share on Romania's 2022 share of world PPP output; the fully paired 2022 figure is 0.0187%.
- **Boundary smearing.** 1899–1937 figures add the Ostrov–Băneasa strip (Durostor county, 1913–40) to the Constanța county of the day (±10k); the INS current-territory series does not isolate 1930.
- **Double-count risk.** RO225 Tulcea (northern Dobruja's Roman cities Troesmis, Noviodunum, Aegyssus and Halmyris; the Danube delta fisheries), RO312 Călărași (the Danube tells opposite Hârșova and Ostrov), Bulgarian Dobrich and Silistra (Durostorum, Dionysopolis, the Varna horizon).
- **Citation flags.** No printed population estimate for Roman Dobruja was retrieved; the c. 200 CE numerator is built from walled areas and the epigraphic vicus list. The 1899 and 1912 county totals are from memory of the census volumes and are flagged for verification.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 in Window 1 and, at the top of range only, c. 200 and c. 400 CE; band 9 held c. 150–450 CE on GDP; band 7 on GDP 1909–1914, 1977 and 2022–2023.
