# RO424 · Timiș County (Județul Timiș), Romania

**UNIT** RO424 (NUTS-3) = Timiș County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 8,697 km², Romania's largest county (under the 10,000 km² smearing flag, but noted); seat Timișoara; municipalities Timișoara and Lugoj; towns Buziaș, Ciacova, Deta, Făget, Gătaia, Jimbolia, Recaș, Sânnicolau Mare; 89 communes. Historic components, in 1876–1918 units: (a) the central and northern districts of Temes County (Temesvár city, Központi, Csák, Detta, Buziásfürdő and Rékas districts), i.e. Temes less its Vršac, Bela Crkva and Kovin districts (now Serbia) and less its Lipova, Vinga and Aradul Nou districts (now Arad County); (b) the Romanian part of Torontál County (Sânnicolau Mare, Periam, Jimbolia and Cenei districts); (c) the Lugoj, Făget and Bega (Balinț) districts of Krassó-Szörény County with the town of Lugoj. Interwar: Timiș-Torontal County (499,443 in 1930) less the Lipova, Vinga and Aradul Nou plăși, plus the Lugoj, Făget, Balinț and Margina plăși of Severin County (239,586 in 1930 for the whole county). 1950–1968 the territory sat inside the Timișoara Region (polygon not used). Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: the end of the Banat/Vinča tells and the Early Copper Age (Tiszapolgár) dispersal across the Banat plain.
**Documented-era peak** Window 12, c. 1900 (Hungarian census county totals apportioned to the polygon); the 1850 moment on Willcox's floor ties on both bands (Ruling D: earliest attainment 1850).

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 8,697 km² (INS), almost all lowland: the Banat plain of the Timiș, Bega, Bârzava and Aranca, with the Lipova and Poiana Ruscă foothills in the east (~10–15%). Pre-drainage marsh and floodplain covered perhaps a tenth of the plain; Neolithic settlement sat on the terraces and levees inside those wetlands. Usable: 7,500–8,000 km².
2. Cultures at the moment: the last Banat/Vinča tells (Parța, Uivar, Foeni, Hodoni, Sânandrei) end c. 4600–4500 BCE and the Early Copper Age Tiszapolgár pattern of dispersed hamlets follows, exactly as on the Hungarian side of the plain. Uivar is the DFG-excavated reference tell for the north-eastern Vinča periphery (Schier & Drașovean); Parța holds the best-preserved Neolithic sanctuary of the Carpathian Basin.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied. → 15,000–24,000, reported 15–25k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: treating the 60-odd known Banat tells as contemporaneous at 4500 BCE and summing them at tell densities (Uivar's ~10 enclosed hectares at 50–100/ha, or the house-accumulation models of Porčić for Uivar, Gomolava and Divostin). The tells were abandoned at the Vinča–Tiszapolgár transition.
- Killed: any county figure above ~30,000 (>3.5/km² of the whole polygon), which no regional synthesis supports.
- Survives: 25,000 people means ~100 hamlets of 250 or ~250 of 100 at 5–9 km spacing; cropland need at Neolithic yields is under 3% of the usable area; the marsh was fishing and fowling ground, credited through the high-end density rather than as empty land.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Deep score — Window 12, c. 1900 (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Three apportionments of the 1900 Hungarian census county totals to the polygon: (i) by district, Temes 437,039 × ~0.55 (Temesvár 53,033 plus the five central districts) + Torontál 591,619 × ~0.20 (the Romanian districts) + Krassó-Szörény 443,001 × ~0.22 (Lugoj town and the Lugoj, Făget and Bega districts) ≈ 455k; (ii) the 1930 Romanian census retrospective for the interwar Timiș-Torontal territory, 454,121 in 1900, less the Arad plăși (~65k) plus the Severin plăși (~95k) ≈ 484k; (iii) the alternative retrospective series for the same territory (513,350 in 1900) adjusted the same way ≈ 543k. Lawyered range 460–540k.
2. GDP per head: Hungary against the world 1.33 (Maddison: Hungary $1,682 vs world $1,262 in 1900) × county index 1.0–1.3 (Temes was among the richest grain counties of Hungary and Timișoara its chief southern industrial city; the Lugoj and Făget hill districts were poorer) → 1.33–1.73× world.

Denominator: 1,564M (Maddison 2003), the lowest published 1900 total (Willcox 1,571M; McEvedy & Jones 1,625M; UN 1,650M).

Result: population 0.0294–0.0345% (band 8); GDP 0.039–0.060% (band 8–9, 9 at the lawyered top). The 1910 moment (490–550k over 1,739M; Temes 500,835, Torontál 615,151, Krassó-Szörény 466,147) gives 0.028–0.032% and 0.039–0.057%, the same bands.

## Candidates not adopted as the record
- **Window 2, c. 1300 BCE, Cornești-Iarcuri.** The largest prehistoric fortification in Europe: 17.65 km² inside the outer of four concentric ramparts, built in the fifteenth–fourteenth centuries BCE and burned near the end of the millennium; magnetometry and surface survey show dwellings over large areas and "a city-like settlement" (Szentmiklosi, Heeb, Heeb, Harding, Krause & Becker, *Antiquity* 85, 2011). No published population figure. Lawyered: 5–25k on the site (the two inner enclosures, ~270 ha, at 20–40/ha up to the "town" reading) plus 8–16k of Cruceni–Belegiš settlement across the rest of the county → 13–40k over 43.1M → 0.030–0.093% population, 0.024–0.111% GDP, band 8–10. Veto: the whole 1,765 ha at village densities (35–70k) is killed because the outer enclosures are largely empty of houses.
- **Window 4, c. 50 BCE, Dacian allocation.** Burebista's Dacia at 1.5–2.0M (Strabo VII.3.13 read at 1:8–1:10); the polygon is 2.9% of it by area and all plain, allocation 2.5–3.5% → 37–70k over the Deevey-deep 137M → 0.027–0.051% population, 0.022–0.061% GDP, band 7–9. Allocation-grade, so not the record.
- **Window 8, c. 1320, the royal seat.** 50–90k over 358M with the royal-seat premium (1.6–2.0 argued × Eastern Europe 0.9) → 0.020–0.045% GDP, band 7–8. **Window 9, c. 1550, the eyalet capital.** 60–100k over 485M → 0.018–0.037%, band 7–8. **Window 10, c. 1650 on Willcox's 470M.** 40–80k (the 1717 Habsburg conscription found the whole Banat near-empty, ~20,000 houses) → 0.012–0.031%, band 6–8.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 8.** Nameable command: Timișoara was the residence of King Charles I of Hungary in 1315–1323, the kingdom's de facto capital; John Hunyadi's base as Count of Temes (1441–1456); the capital of the Ottoman Eyalet of Temeşvar (1552–1716), the principal fortress of the Ottoman north-west; and the capital of the Habsburg Banat of Temeswar crown land (1718–1778) with its Military Frontier command; sieges of 1551–52, 1716 and 1849. A royal and provincial seat rather than a global chokepoint, so 8, not 9.
- **Mind 6.** The Parța Neolithic sanctuary (c. 5000 BCE); Timișoara's firsts (the first electric street lighting in continental Europe, 1884; the first horse tram in Hungary, 1869); the Banat's multiconfessional settlement model; the Polytechnic (1920). The 1989 revolution's start and the 2023 Capital of Culture fall under the event-fame ban.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is the Window 1 floor artefact; the open-top extension (12–13) is driven by the polygon's size, not by anything special about the Banat at 4500 BCE. The documented-era record is shared by 1850 and 1900 on both bands; the 1900 numerator spans 455–543k across three apportionment methods, and only the upper part of the GDP range reaches band 9.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 only (~5×). The 1900 GDP figure is at most 1.5× consensus.
- **Boundary smearing.** Romania's largest county, assembled from three pre-1918 counties and two interwar ones; every figure before 1948 is a reconstruction, and the 1968 transfers to Arad (Lipova, Vinga, Aradul Nou) and from Severin (Lugoj, Făget, Balinț, Margina) are estimated at ±10k each.
- **Double-count risk.** RO421 Arad (the Lipova, Vinga and Aradul Nou districts of Temes), RO422 Caraș-Severin (the rest of Krassó-Szörény and Severin), and the Serbian Banat (Vršac, Bela Crkva, Kovin, the Torontál remainder), which is not on the list. The Dacian allocation must sum to ≤100% of Dacia across Romanian units.
- **Not counted.** Starčevo–Criș and the Vinča tells' floruit (before Window 1); the transient garrison and administrative populations of the eyalet and the crown land (C8).
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1; band 8 population held 1850–1910; band 7 GDP held 1930–1977 and again in 2022 on the PPP basis, dipping to 6 in 1992–2011.
