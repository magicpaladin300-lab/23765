# SD-RS · Red Sea State (Wilayat al-Bahr al-Ahmar), Sudan

**UNIT** SD-RS (ISO 3166-2) = Red Sea State. Scale flag (C14): an ADM1 unit of 218,887 km², thirty to forty times a NUTS-3 county; every share below carries the polygon-size caveat.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (state of 1994): 218,887 km², seat Port Sudan; localities Port Sudan, Suakin, Sinkat, Tokar, Haya, Derudeb, Gebeit al-Maadin, Halaib, Agig, Dordeib. Sudan's whole Red Sea coast from the disputed Hala'ib triangle to the Eritrean border, the Red Sea Hills and the Nubian Desert's eastern slope, the Tokar delta of the Gash and the Beja country. Historic components: the Beja (Blemmyes) lands and the Wadi Allaqi gold fields, the ports of Ptolemais Theron, Badi, Aydhab and Suakin, the Ottoman and Egyptian Suakin, the condominium's Red Sea Province (Port Sudan from 1905–09), Kassala Province (1956–74) and Eastern Region (1974–94). Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: the Red Sea Hills' humid-period herders and the coast's khors at the equivalence-rule densities.
**Documented-era peak** Window 7, c. 1100–1250: Aydhab, the Hajj and India-trade port of Fatimid, Ayyubid and early Mamluk Egypt (Nasir Khusraw 1050, al-Idrisi, Ibn Jubayr 1183, Ibn Battuta 1326), with the Beja gold of Wadi Allaqi and the Badi and Suakin anchorages; band 10 at the lawyered top (range 7–10). Census-grade peak: band 7 (population 2008–2024, Port Sudan as the wartime capital since 2023).

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 218,887 km²: the Red Sea Hills (~90,000 km²) with their khors and acacia pastures, the coastal plain and the Tokar delta (~10,000 km² of better-watered ground), and the Nubian Desert's eastern slope (~120,000 km²).
2. Communities at the moment: the Red Sea Hills' pastoral Neolithic (the Atbai ceramic tradition, c. 5000–3000 BCE, is the attested horizon; the Gash Group lies south-west in Kassala) and the coast's shell-midden fishers; the equivalence rule for a non-literate society applies at the high end.
3. Density, equivalence rule, high end: 0.1–0.3 persons/km² on the coast and delta, 0.02–0.1 on the hills, 0.01–0.04 on the desert slope (the drier end of the Saharan Neolithic range, C14). → 3.2–14k.
4. GDP multiplier 0.8–1.0 (pastoral, with coastal fishing).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: savanna densities on the hills; killed a total above ~16,000 (>0.07/km² over the whole state).
- Survives: 14,000 people means ~70–140 camps of 100 along the khors and the coast; grazing need under 5% of the polygon.
- Allocation flag: the whole figure is an equivalence-rule allocation; the Atbai sites are undated in detail.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across the humid-period Sahara and its margins gives 1–2 million people against a 5M world floor, a fifth to two fifths of humanity.

## Deep score — Window 7, c. 1100–1250 (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Built numerators: Aydhab, the port where the Qus caravan road met the Red Sea, through which the Egyptian Hajj and the Yemen and India trade passed from the 10th to the 14th century (Nasir Khusraw stayed three months in 1050; al-Idrisi called it a great port; Ibn Jubayr sailed from it in 1183 and described the pilgrim traffic and the Beja boatmen; Ibn Battuta in 1326), 5–15k with its seasonal pilgrim and merchant population; Suakin, already a Beja port, 2–5k; Badi (Rih island) in decline, 1–2k. Towns and ports 8–22k.
2. Countryside: the Beja (Hadariba, Hadendowa, Bisharin) herders of the hills and coast and the Wadi Allaqi gold-washers of the Nubian Desert (the medieval Arab mining of al-Allaqi and al-Shanka, described by al-Ya'qubi and al-Maqrizi), 40–110k at 0.2–0.5 per km² of the hills and desert. County 50–130k.
3. GDP multiplier 1.6–2.4: the Egypt–Red Sea region's per-capita level (~1.0–1.2 of world under the Fatimids) × role premium 1.6–2.0 relative to the region for the entrepôt of the Hajj and the India trade, whose customs the Fatimids and Ayyubids farmed (al-Maqrizi records Aydhab's revenues as a major item), with the gold of Allaqi ("capital/entrepôt argued"; no surviving ledger total, so the 2.0–2.5 band is not claimed).

Denominator: 301M at 1100 (Biraben 1979; C2 deep floor); 348M at 1180 (Ibn Jubayr's visit); 360M at 1250 (the Karimi merchants' heyday).

Result: population 0.017–0.043% (band 7–8); GDP 0.027–0.104% (band 7–10; 0.057% at the mid-range of 90k × 1.9, band 9; 10 at the lawyered top on the 1100 floor). At 1180: 60–150k over 348M → 0.0172–0.0431% population, 0.0276–0.103% GDP (band 7–10); at 1250: 0.0267–0.100% (band 7–10).

Veto notes
- Killed: a county above ~150,000 (the Beja country cannot have carried more than 0.5/km² of the hills); killed a multiplier above 2.4 (no customs total; the India trade's value belonged to the Karimi merchants of Cairo, not to Aydhab).
- Killed: counting Qus and Upper Egypt's share of the route, or Jeddah's.
- Early-source grounding: the travellers describe size and traffic, not population; the town numerator is built from the site's extent (the ruins of Aydhab cover ~1 km of shore) and the pilgrim season.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 8.** Nameable command and chokepoint: Port Sudan, Sudan's only major seaport and oil export terminal (Bashayer), the country's sole maritime gate and since 2023 its de facto capital; Aydhab, the Egyptian Hajj's and the India trade's Red Sea port for three centuries, whose closure in 1426 rerouted the pilgrimage; Suakin, the Ottoman and Egyptian Red Sea port and the base of the Suakin campaigns of 1884–91; the Wadi Allaqi and Ariab gold. Not 9: the chokepoint of the Red Sea itself lies at Bab al-Mandab and Suez, not on this coast.
- **Mind 5.** Nameable but regional: Suakin's coral-stone old town; the ruins of Aydhab and Badi; the Beja (Bedawiyet) language and culture; the Red Sea University. Nothing of national or world intellectual scale.

## Flags
- **Scale (C14).** An ADM1 state of 218,887 km²; at NUTS-3 scale (Port Sudan's locality) its Window 1 share would be a fiftieth and its Aydhab share a quarter. Every band above is a state-level band.
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is a Window 1 allocation over a hyper-arid landscape (band 9 at the bottom of range). The documented-era record is band 10 only at the top of range on the 1100 Biraben floor (130k people and the 2.4× multiplier together), band 9 at the mid-range; the attested moments (1183, 1326) sit on higher floors. The census-grade series is band 7 since 2008.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 (~7–9×); the Aydhab GDP figure (~4×). The contemporary figures are 1.0–1.35× (the 2024 top counts the wartime displaced).
- **Boundary smearing.** Pre-1956 figures are allocations from Kassala and Red Sea provinces; the Hala'ib triangle (disputed with Egypt) is counted as Sudanese throughout; Aydhab itself lies inside the triangle.
- **Double-count risk.** Egypt's Red Sea and Aswan governorates (Wadi Allaqi's upper course, Berenice, the Qus road), SD-KA Kassala (the Gash and the Hadendowa dar), SD-NR River Nile (the Nubian Desert gold), Eritrea's Northern Red Sea (Aqiq's hinterland, Massawa's trade).
- **Citation flags.** The 2018 CBS figure was not retrieved (range from the state lists); no population estimate for Aydhab was retrieved (the town numerator is built from the site and the sources' description); the 2024 population and the GDP shares are estimates.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 in Window 1 and, at the top of range only, c. 1050–1300; band 9 c. 1000–1400 on the lawyered Aydhab figures; band 7 on population since 2008.
