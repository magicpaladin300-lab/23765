# SD-DS · South Darfur State (Wilayat Janub Darfur), Sudan

**UNIT** SD-DS (ISO 3166-2) = South Darfur State. Scale flag (C14): an ADM1 unit of ~72,000 km², ten to fifteen times a NUTS-3 county; every share below carries the polygon-size caveat.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (since the creation of East Darfur in January 2012): about 72,300 km² (the pre-2012 state had 127,300 km², of which East Darfur took ~55,000), seat Nyala; localities include Nyala North and South, Beleil, Kas, Mershing, Nitega, Shattaya, Kubum, Buram, Tulus, Gereida, Rehed al-Birdi, Ed al-Fursan, Katila, Dimso, Um Dafug, Sunta and Al-Radoum. The southern slopes of Jebel Marra and the Baggara savanna belt from Kas to the Bahr al-Arab, with 400–800 mm of rain. Historic components: the Keira sultanate's southern dars (Dar Fongoro, the Baggara Habbaniya, Beni Halba, Ta'aisha and Fallata), the Southern Darfur District of the condominium and of Darfur Province (1956–94), then South Darfur State (1994). Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: the African Humid Period savanna and Jebel Marra's southern slopes at the equivalence-rule densities.
**Documented-era peak** Window 16, 2018–2024: the Central Bureau of Statistics projection of 5,353,025 (2018), the second-largest state population in Sudan, carried to 6.0–6.4M in 2024 with Nyala (492,984 in 2008; well over a million with the displaced) as Darfur's metropolis; band 9 (census-grade on the CBS projection). The first unit in the gallery whose documented-era record is a contemporary population share.

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area ~72,300 km²; Jebel Marra's southern slopes and foot-hills (~3,000 km²) with volcanic soils and 600–1,000 mm of rain; the Baggara savanna (~69,000 km²), wooded grassland in the African Humid Period when today's 400–800 mm belt lay further north and the Bahr al-Arab plain was wetter still.
2. Communities at the moment: Sahelian Neolithic herders and cultivators; southern Darfur is archaeologically almost unexplored, so the equivalence rule for a non-literate society applies at the high end.
3. Density, equivalence rule, high end: 0.8–1.5 persons/km² on the massif's slopes, 0.15–0.4 persons/km² on the savanna (Hassan's Saharan–Sahelian Neolithic range), as for Central Darfur (C14). → 12,800–32,000, reported 12.8–32k.
4. GDP multiplier 0.8–1.0 (pastoral-agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: European farmed-zone densities anywhere; killed a total above ~40,000 (>0.55/km² over the whole state).
- Survives: 32,000 people means ~160–320 camps and hamlets of 100 along the wadis and on the slopes; grazing and cropland need under 5% of the polygon.
- Allocation flag: no excavated site in the polygon anchors the figure.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across the humid-period Sahara and Sahel gives 1–2 million people against a 5M world floor, a fifth to two fifths of humanity; with Europe's farmed zone at the same stacking the two regions exceed the floor. The floor artefact is at its most extreme for large pastoral polygons.

## Deep score — Window 16, 2018–2024 (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Population: the 2008 census counted 4,069,300 in the pre-2012 South Darfur (4,093,594 in the final tables), of which the localities now in East Darfur held 0.8–0.9M, leaving 3.2–3.3M on the current polygon; the Central Bureau of Statistics projection for the current state is 5,353,025 in 2018 (second in Sudan after Khartoum), with East Darfur projected separately at 1,119,451. Carried on the CBS growth of ~2.5% a year, 6.0–6.4M in 2024; the war of 2023– has brought displaced people into Nyala and the camps (Kalma, Otash, Dereig) and driven others out, so the 2024 figure is an estimate on the official trend.
2. No GDP multiplier: contemporary population shares are direct.

Denominators: 6,736M (2008), 7,616M (2018), 8,160M (2024) (UN series; rubric floors).

Result: population 0.0475–0.0490% (2008; band 8, 9 at the line), 0.0703% (2018; band 9), 0.074–0.078% (2024; band 9).

## Flags
- **Scale (C14).** An ADM1 state of ~72,300 km²; at NUTS-3 scale its shares would be a tenth to a fifteenth. Every band above is a state-level band.
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is a Window 1 density allocation on an unexcavated landscape. The documented-era record is census-grade only through the CBS projection (the last census is 2008); the 2024 figure is a trend estimate across a war that has moved hundreds of thousands in and out of the state.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 (~7–8×); the pre-1956 figures (~2.5×). The contemporary figures are 1.0–1.1×.
- **Boundary smearing.** The 2008 and earlier figures are allocations from the pre-2012 state (East Darfur's localities removed), from Darfur Province (1956–93) and from the sultanate; the current area is itself a derivation (127,300 less ~55,000).
- **Double-count risk.** SD-DE East Darfur (the 2008 parent state; the Rizeigat dar), SD-DC Central Darfur (Jebel Marra's slopes; Kas and Nertiti), SD-DW West Darfur, South Sudan's Northern Bahr el Ghazal (the Bahr al-Arab frontier).
- **Citation flags.** The current area and the 2008 current-territory population are derivations; the 2024 population and the GDP shares are estimates; no sultanate population total was retrieved.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1; band 9 held on population from c. 2010 on the CBS trend; band 8 from the 1990s.
