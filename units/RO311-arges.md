# RO311 · Argeș County (Județul Argeș), Romania

**UNIT** RO311 (NUTS-3) = Argeș County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 6,826 km², seat Pitești; municipalities Pitești, Câmpulung, Curtea de Argeș; towns Costești, Mioveni, Ștefănești, Topoloveni; 95 communes. The Făgăraș and Iezer mountains in the north (~25%), the Muscel Subcarpathians, the Getic piedmont and the Pitești plain. Historic components: the interwar counties of Argeș (seat Pitești) and Muscel (seat Câmpulung), with small exchanges towards Olt, Dâmbovița and Vâlcea. 1950–1968 the territory was the core of the Argeș Region (polygon not used). Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: Gumelnița–Sălcuța settlement of the Argeș plain and piedmont.
**Documented-era peak** Window 9, c. 1500: Câmpulung as Wallachia's leading trading town in the Brașov customs register of 1503, with Curtea de Argeș's court and monastery; band 8 at the lawyered top (range 6–8). Census-grade plateau 1850–1977 (band 7).

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 6,826 km² (INS); the mountain quarter is excluded: usable plain, piedmont and hill valleys 4,800–5,200 km².
2. Cultures at the moment: Gumelnița on the Argeș and Dâmbovnic plains (Teiu, Ziduri, the Popești terraces) meeting Sălcuța in the piedmont; the Muscel valleys thinly settled.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied. → 9,600–15,600, reported 10–15k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: farming densities on the Făgăraș and Iezer; killed any county total above ~21,000 (>4/km² usable).
- Survives: 15,000 people means ~75–150 villages and hamlets of 100–200 along the Argeș, Vedea, Teleorman and Dâmbovnic at 5–8 km spacing; cropland need under 3% of the usable area.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Deep score — Window 9, c. 1500 (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Câmpulung, one of only three Wallachian towns where Brașov's merchants were allowed to sell (with Târgșor and Târgoviște) and the largest of all in the Brașov twentieth register of 1503, whose trade exceeded Târgșor's almost one million aspers; Curtea de Argeș, Wallachia's first princely seat (Basarab I, c. 1330) and first metropolitan see (1359), with Neagoe Basarab's monastery under construction (1512–17); Pitești as a customs point on the Brașov road. Towns 6–10k, villages 29–45k → 35–55k.
2. GDP multiplier. Schedule band 2.0–2.5, "quantified fiscal/commercial documentation", named source: the Brașov vigesima register of 1503 (Manolescu 1965; Pakucs-Willcocks 2005), in which Câmpulung heads the Wallachian towns. Per C12 the premium is applied relative to the region and multiplied by Maddison's Eastern Europe ratio (0.88–1.0) → 1.76–2.5. Câmpulung is 12–18% of the county, between Bistrița's and Brașov's shares, so the top of the band is thin.

Denominator: 425M at 1500 (McEvedy & Jones).

Result: population 0.0082–0.0129% (band 6); GDP 0.0145–0.0324% (band 6–8; 8 at the lawyered top).

Veto notes
- Killed: a Ruling A piercing above 2.5×; Câmpulung's registered trade (over a million aspers, roughly 20,000+ florins) is a few tenths of the county's agrarian product at most.
- Killed: counting Brașov's own merchants' turnover at Câmpulung as the county's; only the Wallachian side of the exchange and the town's margin are the county's.

## Earlier candidate — Window 8, c. 1350, the first capitals
Câmpulung (the Laurencius tombstone of 1300, the earliest in Wallachia; the Saxon-Hungarian town) and Curtea de Argeș as Basarab I's and Nicolae Alexandru's seat, with the metropolitan see from 1359: 30–50k over 360M with the capital premium (1.6–2.0 × 0.9–1.0) → 0.012–0.028% GDP, band 6–7 (8 at the edge). Not the record: the court moved to Târgoviște c. 1396–1418 and the towns were small.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 7.** Nameable command and commodity: the cradle seats of Wallachia at Câmpulung and Curtea de Argeș (c. 1300–1396); the Rucăr–Bran road and the Transalutanus terminus at Jidava; the Dacia works at Mioveni (1968), Romania's largest industrial exporter, with the Pitești refinery. A principality's first capital and a national-scale industry, so 7.
- **Mind 7.** Nameable institutions: Neagoe Basarab's monastery at Curtea de Argeș (1517), the royal necropolis of Romania's kings and the setting of the Master Manole legend; the first Wallachian Metropolis (1359); the Laurencius tombstone (1300), the oldest in Wallachia; Câmpulung's Saxon-Hungarian town and its early schools. The Pitești "experiment" of 1949–51 falls under the event-fame ban.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is the Window 1 floor artefact. The documented-era record (c. 1500) rests on the schedule premium for a town that is an eighth to a sixth of the county; band 8 holds only at the top of range, and the census-grade plateau (1850–1977) is a steady band 7.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 only (~4–5×); the c. 1500 figure is ~2–3×.
- **Boundary smearing.** Pre-1930 figures are reconstructions from the Argeș and Muscel counties (±10k).
- **Double-count risk.** RO122 Brașov (the Brașov merchants' side of the 1503 trade and the Bran pass), RO313 Dâmbovița and RO316 Prahova (the other register towns), RO317 Teleorman and RO414 Olt (the Transalutanus and Alutanus lines).
- **Citation flags.** Câmpulung's own 1503 total was not read (only its rank above Târgșor's ~1M aspers); the 1850 and 1912 totals are reconstructions.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1; band 7 held c. 1850–1977 on population and 1850–1912 and 1977 on GDP.
