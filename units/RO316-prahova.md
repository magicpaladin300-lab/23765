# RO316 · Prahova County (Județul Prahova), Romania

**UNIT** RO316 (NUTS-3) = Prahova County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 4,716 km², seat Ploiești; municipalities Ploiești and Câmpina; towns Azuga, Băicoi, Boldești-Scăeni, Breaza, Bușteni, Comarnic, Mizil, Plopeni, Sinaia, Slănic, Urlați, Vălenii de Munte; 90 communes. The Ploiești plain in the south, the Subcarpathian oil hills in the middle, the Prahova valley and the Bucegi–Ciucaș mountains in the north. Historic components: the interwar Prahova County (seat Ploiești) with the Mizil area taken from Buzău County and small adjustments towards Dâmbovița; before 1845 the eastern hills belonged to the Saac (Săcuieni) county. 1950–1968 the territory was the Ploiești Region's core (polygon not used). Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: Boian–Gumelnița settlement of the Ploiești plain and the Subcarpathian valleys.
**Documented-era peak** Window 13, c. 1937: the Romanian oil industry at its height, fourth in the world in 1935–36, with Prahova holding most of the fields and almost all of the refining. Population record 1930 (band 7, held c. 1900–1992).

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 4,716 km² (INS). The Bucegi, Baiu, Ciucaș and Grohotiș mountains and the higher Subcarpathian ridges take 25–35%; usable plain, terraces and hill valleys 3,000–3,500 km².
2. Cultures at the moment: Boian–Gumelnița on the Prahova, Teleajen and Cricov terraces (Târgșoru Vechi, Ploiești-Triaj, Budureasca), with the Subcarpathian valleys thinly settled.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied. → 6,000–10,500, reported 6–10k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: farming densities on the mountain third and on the high Subcarpathian ridges.
- Killed: any county total above ~14,000 (>4/km² usable).
- Survives: 10,000 people means ~50–100 villages and hamlets of 100–200 along three river corridors at 5–8 km spacing; cropland need under 3% of the usable area.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Deep score — Window 13, c. 1937 (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Population on the current territory: 472,249 in 1930 and 557,776 in 1948 (INS retrospective series), so 505–520k in 1937.
2. GDP multiplier, built per C12 as Romania's ratio to the world × the county's index to Romania. Romania 1938 $1,242 vs world ≈ $1,790 per head (Maddison 2003) → 0.69. County index 2.2–3.2: Romania produced 8.78 million tons of crude in 1936, the inter-war maximum, and ranked fourth among world producers in 1935–36; oil was the largest industrial branch and about two fifths of exports by value, of the order of 7–10% of GDP with refining and transport; Prahova held the Băicoi–Țintea, Boldești, Câmpina–Bușteni and Ariceștii fields (half to two thirds of national crude) and the Ploiești and Câmpina refineries (four fifths or more of refining), so the oil sector alone put 5–6% of Romania's GDP inside a county with 2.6% of its population, before Ploiești's other industry, the Prahova valley resorts and the Slănic salt mine. → 0.69 × 2.2–3.2 = 1.52–2.21.

Denominator: 2,212M at 1937 (rubric points 1918 and 1950, interpolated).

Result: population 0.0228–0.0235% (band 7); GDP 0.0347–0.0520% (band 8; 9 only at the extreme top). Neighbouring moments: 1930, 0.0230% population and 0.030–0.050% GDP (band 8); 1912 (370–390k reconstructed, Romania 1.14 × county 1.3–1.8 with Câmpina's Steaua Română refinery, Europe's largest at 1897, and Romania fifth in world output in 1913), 0.021–0.022% and 0.031–0.045% (band 8); 1948, 0.0227% and 0.023–0.032% (band 7–8).

Veto notes
- Killed: a Ruling B valuation of the county's crude at world prices as the record. 4.4–6.1 million tons of Prahova crude in 1936–38 at the 1930s wellhead price (~$1.1 a barrel) is $40–55 million of the day, roughly $0.4–0.5 billion in 1990 dollars, 0.010–0.012% of a $4.3–4.5 trillion world; with refining and the non-oil county product it reaches 0.024–0.030%, band 7. The route is honest but the 1930s oil price makes it bind below the index route, so the index route is the lawyered figure and this is the consensus column.
- Killed: crediting Moreni–Gura Ocniței (Dâmbovița) crude refined at Ploiești to Prahova; only the refining margin on it counts here (double-count rule).

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 9.** Nameable commodity and command: the Ploiești–Câmpina oil complex. Romania was fourth in world crude output in 1935–36 and the principal external oil source of the Axis in 1940–44; the refineries around Ploiești supplied a third of Germany's high-octane fuel, which made them the target of Operation Tidal Wave (1 August 1943) and the 1944 oil campaign. The 1857 Râfov refinery was the world's first industrial oil refinery. Below Los Alamos's sole command (10), level with Suez (9) for the war years.
- **Mind 6.** The birthplace of industrial refining (1857) and of Romanian petroleum engineering (Câmpina school, 1904; the Ploiești oil institute); Peleș Castle at Sinaia, the royal summer residence and seat of the crown's decisions 1883–1947. Caragiale's birth at Haimanale and the 1870 "Republic of Ploiești" fall under the event-fame ban.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is the Window 1 floor artefact. The documented-era record is c. 1937 by a short head over 1930 and 1912, all band 8 on the GDP pathway; band 9 appears only at the extreme top of the 1937 range, on a county index of 3.2.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 only (~4–5×). The 1937 GDP figure is ~1.5× consensus.
- **Boundary smearing.** Figures before 1930 are reconstructions; the Prahova share of national crude (half to two thirds) is an estimate for 1936–38, since Moreni lies in Dâmbovița: in 1931 Moreni (33.6%) and Gura Ocniței (29.3%), both in Dâmbovița, held 63% of Romanian output, so Prahova's share should be read as two fifths to three fifths and the band-9 top of its 1937 range as the thinnest part of the claim.
- **Double-count risk.** RO313 Dâmbovița (Moreni–Gura Ocniței crude refined at Ploiești), RO222 Buzău (the Mizil area and the Buzău fields), RO122 Brașov (the Prahova valley–Predeal corridor, scored there as a pass).
- **Citation flags.** The 1912 county total on the current territory and the 2022 GDP are reconstructed from shares; the oil-sector share of Romanian GDP is an order-of-magnitude figure.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1; band 8 GDP held c. 1905–1948 and touched again in 1977; band 7 population held c. 1900–1992.
