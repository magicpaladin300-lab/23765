# SD-KA · Kassala State (Wilayat Kassala), Sudan

**UNIT** SD-KA (ISO 3166-2) = Kassala State. Scale flag (C14): an ADM1 unit of 36,710 km², five to eight times a NUTS-3 county; every share below carries the polygon-size caveat.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (state of 1994): 36,710 km², seat Kassala; localities Kassala, Rural Kassala, West Kassala, Khashm el-Girba, New Halfa, Aroma, Hamashkoreib, Wad al-Hilaiw, Rural Aroma, North Delta, Telkuk. The Gash (Mareb) delta under the Taka hills, the Atbara valley with the Khashm el-Girba reservoir and the New Halfa scheme, and the Beja (Hadendowa, Beni Amer) plains to the Eritrean border. Historic components: the Gash Group's Mahal Teglinos, the Beja country, the Funj and Ethiopian marches, the Egyptian Kassala (1840), Mahdist and Italian Kassala (1885–97), the condominium's Kassala Province and Eastern Region (1974–94). Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: the Butana Group and Saroba communities of the Atbara and Gash and the humid-period plains at the equivalence-rule densities.
**Documented-era peak** Window 2, c. 2300 BCE: the Gash Group at Mahal Teglinos (flourishing c. 2800–2500 and 1900–1700 BCE, with its stelae, storerooms and Egyptian, Kerma and Arabian imports), the leading archaeological candidate for the hinterland of Punt, whose products the Palermo Stone records Sahure's expedition bringing to Egypt (80,000 measures of myrrh, 6,000 of electrum); band 10 at the lawyered top (range 9–10; open-top extension 11 at the very top); c. 1700 BCE ties. Census-grade peak: band 8 (population 2018–2024).

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 36,710 km²: the Atbara and Gash valleys and the Gash delta (~4,000 km²) and the Beja plains and hills (~32,000 km²), savanna in the African Humid Period.
2. Communities at the moment: the Butana Group of the Atbara (Khashm el-Girba area, c. 4500–3500 BCE, among the earliest herding communities of the eastern Sudan) and the Saroba phase of the Gash delta (c. 5000–3700 BCE); the equivalence rule for a non-literate society applies at the high end.
3. Density, equivalence rule, high end: 0.5–1.5 persons/km² on the valleys and delta (the Butana Group's large sites), 0.15–0.4 on the plains (Hassan's Saharan–Sahelian Neolithic range, C14). → 2–6k on the valleys, 4.8–12.8k on the plains, 6.8–18.8k in all.
4. GDP multiplier 0.8–1.1 (herder-cultivators with cattle wealth).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes

- Survives: 18,800 people means ~95–190 camps and hamlets of 100 along the Atbara, the Gash and the plains' khors; grazing and cropland need under 5% of the polygon.

## Deep score — Window 2, c. 2300 BCE (documented-era peak; c. 1700 BCE ties)

Numerator components
1. Towns and villages 4–10k.
2. Countryside: the Gash and Atbara valleys' cultivators and the Beja ancestors' herds at 0.3–0.8 per km² of the valleys and 0.1–0.3 on the plains; 8–25k. County 12–35k.
3. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.6: the Egypt–Nubia–Red Sea region's per-capita level (~1.0–1.2 of a Bronze Age world) × role premium 1.0–1.4 relative to the region for the incense, gold, electrum and ebony trade that the Palermo Stone quantifies for Sahure's Punt expedition (c. 2450 BCE: 80,000 measures of myrrh, 6,000 of electrum, 2,600 staves) and that Pepi II's and the Middle Kingdom's expeditions continued; the Gash Group's identification with Punt's hinterland (Fattovich) is a hypothesis, so the quantified-source band is not claimed in full.

## Flags
- **Scale (C14).** An ADM1 state of 36,710 km²; at NUTS-3 scale (the Gash delta locality) its shares would be a fifth to an eighth. Every band above is a state-level band.
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is a Window 1 allocation anchored on the Butana Group. The documented-era record (c. 2300 BCE) is extension 11 only at the top of range (35k people and the 1.6× multiplier together), extension 10 at the mid-range; it rests on the Gash Group's identification with Punt's hinterland, which is a hypothesis; c. 1700 BCE ties. The census-grade series reaches band 8 from 2018.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 (~6–8×); the c. 2300 BCE GDP figure (~4×). The contemporary figures are 1.0–1.1×.
- **Boundary smearing.** Pre-1994 figures are allocations from Kassala Province and Eastern Region; the Beja figures are allocations of a nomad country without counts.
- **Double-count risk.** SD-GD Gedaref (the Atbara's right bank and the Butana), SD-RS Red Sea (the Beja country's north, Aydhab's hinterland), Eritrea's Gash-Barka and Anseba (the Gash's upper course, the Beni Amer), Egypt's Punt receipts (counted here only as the polygon's product share).
- **Citation flags.** Mahal Teglinos's size and population are inferred from the published extent (~10 ha); the Palermo Stone quantities are Egypt's receipts; the 2024 population and the GDP shares are estimates.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 in Window 1 and, at the top of range, c. 2800–1700 BCE; band 9 c. 2500–1700 BCE on the lawyered Gash Group figures; band 8 on population from 2018.
