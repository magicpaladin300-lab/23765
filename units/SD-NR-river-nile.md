# SD-NR · River Nile State (Wilayat Nahr an-Nil), Sudan

**UNIT** SD-NR (ISO 3166-2) = River Nile State. Scale flag (C14): an ADM1 unit of 122,123 km², twenty to twenty-five times a NUTS-3 county; every share below carries the polygon-size caveat.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (state of 1994): 122,123 km², seat Ed Damer; localities Ed Damer, Atbara, Berber, Abu Hamad, Shendi, Al-Matamma, Al-Buhaira. The Nile from the Fourth Cataract bend at Abu Hamad through Berber, Atbara and Shendi to the Sixth Cataract at Sabaloka, the Atbara confluence, the Bayuda desert to the west and the Butana (the Island of Meroe) to the east. Historic components: the heartland of Meroitic Kush (Meroe, Naqa, Musawwarat es-Sufra, Wad Ban Naqa, Dangeil), Alodia's northern reach, the Ja'aliyin and Shendi market, the Turco-Egyptian Berber mudiriya, the condominium's Berber and Northern provinces, Northern Province (1956–94). Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: the Khartoum Neolithic and Butana Group communities of the Nile strip and the humid-period Butana and Bayuda at the equivalence-rule densities.
**Documented-era peak** Window 4, c. 1 CE: Meroe, capital of the Kingdom of Kush (c. 300 BCE–350 CE), at its apogee under Amanirenas, Amanishakheto and Natakamani, with the Island of Meroe's temple cities, the iron industry and a Meroitic Kush put at 1,150,000 people; band 10 (open-top extension 11–13) on GDP, the gallery's second-highest documented-era share. Census-grade peak: band 7 (population 2008–2024).

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 122,123 km²: the Nile strip from Abu Hamad to Sabaloka, ~500 km of river with a usable floodplain of 4,000–5,000 km²; the Butana (~70,000 km²), a summer-rain steppe with wadis and the Shaqadud and Butana Group sites; the Bayuda (~47,000 km²), drier.
2. Communities at the moment: the Khartoum Neolithic (Shaheinab horizon) on the Nile, the Butana Group of the Atbara and the Shaqadud cave sequence in the Butana, herder-fishers and incipient cultivators; the equivalence rule for a non-literate society applies at the high end.
3. Density, equivalence rule, high end: 2–4 persons/km² on the Nile strip, 0.03–0.1 on the Butana and 0.02–0.06 on the Bayuda (the Saharan–Sahelian Neolithic range, C14). → 8–20k on the river, 3.5–11.7k on the steppes, 11.5–31.7k in all.
4. GDP multiplier 0.8–1.1 (riverine herder-cultivators with cattle wealth).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: Egyptian floodplain densities on the Nubian strip; killed steppe densities above 0.1/km²; killed a total above ~38,000.
- Survives: 31,700 people means ~160–320 villages, camps and hamlets of 100, two thirds on the river at 2–4 km spacing; the Shaheinab-type and Butana Group site lists support the riverine figure.
- Allocation flag: a third of the numerator is a steppe allocation.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking along the Nile and across the humid-period Sahara and Sahel gives 2–3 million people against a 5M world floor, roughly half of humanity; the floor artefact is at its most extreme for large polygons.

## Deep score — Window 4, c. 1 CE (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Built numerators: Meroe (Begrawiya), the royal city with the Royal City enclosure, the Amun temple, the iron-slag mounds and the Northern and Southern pyramid cemeteries, 20–30k; the temple cities of the Island of Meroe, Naqa and Musawwarat es-Sufra, Wad Ban Naqa and El-Hassa, 10–20k; Dangeil and the Nile towns of the Berber and Atbara reach 10–20k. Towns 40–70k.
2. Countryside: the Nile strip's villages at 15–30 per km² of floodplain (4,000–5,000 km²), the hafir-watered Butana and the Bayuda herders; the polygon as the capital region holds 13–24% of a Meroitic Kush put at 1,150,000 in the demographic literature. County 150–280k.
3. GDP multiplier 1.6–2.5: the Egypt–Nubia region's per-capita level (~1.2–1.3 of world under the early Roman Empire's Egyptian neighbour) × role premium 1.3–2.0 relative to the region for the capital of Kush, its iron-working (the "Birmingham of Africa"), gold and the Red Sea–Egyptian trade in ivory, ebony and exotic animals that Strabo, Diodorus and Pliny describe ("capital/entrepôt argued"; the sources are literary and epigraphic, so the cap of 2.5 is not exceeded).

Denominator: 133M at 1 CE (Deevey 1960; C2 deep floor; McEvedy & Jones 170M would lower the shares by 22%).

Result: population 0.113–0.21% (band 10, extension 10–11); GDP 0.18–0.53% (band 10; 0.33% at the mid-range of 215k × 2.05, extension 12; extension 13 at the lawyered top). c. 100 CE on the Deevey-interpolated 161M → 0.093–0.174% population, 0.149–0.434% GDP; c. 300 BCE (the royal cemetery moves to Meroe) on 133M: 120–230k × 1.5–2.3 → 0.135–0.40%.

Veto notes
- Killed: a county above ~300,000 (a quarter of the kingdom; Lower Nubia, the Dongola Reach and the Gezira take the rest); killed a multiplier above 2.5 (no fiscal ledger; Ruling A not invoked for the iron and gold).
- Killed: counting Napata and the Dongola Reach (Northern State) or Soba and the Khartoum reach.
- Ancient-source grounding: Herodotus (II.29), Strabo (XVII.1.54, the Roman war of 25–21 BCE with the one-eyed Candace), Diodorus (III), Pliny (VI.35, the Neronian expedition) and the Meroitic royal inscriptions document the kingdom and its seat; the 1,150,000 is a modern demographic estimate for the Meroitic phase, carried at face value.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 7.** Nameable command: Meroe, capital of Kush for six centuries and the state that fought Rome to a treaty (21 BCE); the Island of Meroe's iron, the largest iron-working complex of ancient Africa; Shendi, the caravan market of the eastern Sudan; Atbara, the railway junction and workshop town of the condominium, and the battle of the Atbara (1898); Abu Hamad, the centre of Sudan's gold rush. Not 8: Kush's command never reached beyond the middle Nile after 656 BCE.
- **Mind 8.** Nameable claims of world reference: the Meroitic script (c. 300 BCE), the earliest indigenous alphabetic writing of sub-Saharan Africa; the pyramids of Meroe and the temples of Naqa and Musawwarat es-Sufra (World Heritage); the Candaces of Meroe in Strabo and the Acts of the Apostles; the Great Enclosure of Musawwarat; the Dangeil Amun temple. A script and a royal necropolis of world standing carry the peg to 8.

## Flags
- **Scale (C14).** An ADM1 state of 122,123 km²; at NUTS-3 scale (the Meroe–Shendi reach of 5,000 km²) its Window 1 share would be a quarter and its Meroitic share a half to two thirds. Every band above is a state-level band.
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is a Window 1 density allocation (a third of it steppe). The documented-era record is extension 13 only at the top of range (280k people, a quarter of the published 1,150,000, and the 2.5× multiplier together), extension 12 at the mid-range; c. 300 BCE and c. 100 CE tie within range. The census-grade series is a flat band 7.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 (~6–8×); the c. 1 CE GDP figure (~3–4×). The contemporary figures are 1.0–1.1×.
- **Boundary smearing.** Pre-1956 figures are allocations from Berber and Northern provinces; the Meroitic figure is a share of a kingdom-wide estimate with built town numerators.
- **Double-count risk.** SD-NO Northern (Napata, the Dongola Reach, the Merowe Dam), SD-KH Khartoum (Soba, the Sixth Cataract, Shaqadud's hinterland), SD-GD Gedaref (the Butana's south), SD-KA Kassala (the Atbara's upper course).
- **Citation flags.** The 2018 CBS figure for the state was not retrieved (1.48–1.54M is the projection range in the state lists); the 1,150,000 for Meroitic Kush is the Wikipedia-cited estimate, source not read; the 2024 population and the GDP shares are estimates.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 in Window 1 and c. 300 BCE–200 CE on the lawyered figures (extension 11–13); band 9–10 c. 700 BCE and c. 350; band 7 on population since 2008.
