# HU332 · Békés county (Békés vármegye), Hungary

**UNIT** HU332 (NUTS-3) = Békés county.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (county reform of 1950): 5,629.7 km², seat Békéscsaba; districts Békéscsaba, Békés, Gyomaendrőd, Gyula, Mezőkovácsháza, Orosháza, Sarkad, Szarvas, Szeghalom. The pre-1950 historic county covered 3,670 km²; in 1950 it gained the northern part of Csanád-Arad-Torontál (Battonya, Mezőhegyes, Mezőkovácsháza, Tótkomlós, Elek, Medgyesegyháza, Kunágota, Csanádapáca and smaller villages), south-western Bihar (Sarkad, Okány, Zsadány, Geszt, Mezőgyán, Biharugra, Kötegyán, Méhkerék, Sarkadkeresztúr, Körösnagyharsány, Újszalonta) and Dévaványa with Ecsegfalva from Jász-Nagykun-Szolnok. Boundary divergences of one year or less ignored (none material).

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: Late Neolithic → Early Copper Age transition, Tiszapolgár horizon of the Körös region.
**Documented-era peak** Window 12, c. 1900.
**WINNING PATHWAY** GDP (population share × 1.0–1.2 agrarian default); population pathway practically tied.

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 5,629.7 km² (KSH).
2. Settlement pattern: by the end of the Neolithic the tells were abandoned and the Early Copper Age pattern is "more small settlements distributed evenly across the landscape" (Körös Regional Archaeological Project: Parkinson, Gyucha, Yerkes). Vésztő-Bikeri and Körösladány-Bikeri, c. 4600–4200 cal BC, each a fortified hamlet of ~1–2 ha with ~10 houses, 50–100 people (Parkinson & Gyucha eds., *Bikeri*, 2022).
3. Density pegs from the Late Neolithic tells: Öcsöd-Kováshalom fenced core 136–204 persons/ha, 230–250 people on the 3 ha occupied at once (Raczky, in *The Late Neolithic of the Tisza Region*, 1987); Szeghalom-Kovácshalom 33 ha scatter + 0.5 ha tell (Field Museum / KRAP); Vésztő-Mágor tell 4.25 ha; Polgár-Csőszhalom complex 67.5 ha (scale comparison, outside polygon).
4. Regional density 2–3 persons/km², the high end of regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic farming-zone densities synthesized by Müller (2013, "from 1 to 8 million Europeans, 6000–2000 BCE") and derived by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied (non-literate society, high end). → 11,000–17,000 people.
5. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian): a farming, herding, copper-using society against a world average that was still largely foragers.

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 1978 give 5M at 5000 BCE and 7M at 4000 BCE). No published world total below 5M exists for this date; deep floors do not apply.

Veto notes
- Killed: applying the Öcsöd core density (136–204/ha) to the whole 33 ha of Szeghalom-Kovácshalom (4,500–6,700 people for one site). KRAP's radiocarbon modelling shows the horizontal settlement was not occupied all at once.
- Killed: any county total above ~20,000 (>3.5/km²), which would require tell-grade nucleation that the Early Copper Age record does not show.
- Survives: 3/km² implies hamlets of 50–100 people at 4–6 km spacing, matching the Magyarország Régészeti Topográfiája survey distributions for the Szeghalom, Szarvas and Békés districts; land requirement at Neolithic yields is under 3% of the polygon.

## Deep score — Window 12, c. 1900 (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Historic Békés county: 278,731 (1900 census), 298,710 (1910 census) on 3,670 km² (KSH).
2. Areas annexed in 1950, reconstructed from their own 1900/1910 census totals: ≈118,000 (1900) / ≈125,000 (1910). → Present-territory population ≈395–400k (1900), ≈420–425k (1910). Cross-check: KSH's restated present-territory figure for 1949 is 471,561; 1960: 467,861.
3. GDP per head: Maddison (2003) Hungary, present territory, 1900 = $1,682 vs world $1,262 (1990 int'l $) → 1.33×. Békés relative to Hungary 1.0–1.15: Demeter & Pénzes's 1910 county indices put it at or above the national average on per-capita direct-tax base and crop output (the wheat country of the "Viharsarok"). → 1.33–1.53× world.

Denominator: 1,564M (Maddison 2003), the lowest published 1900 total (Willcox 1931: 1,571M; McEvedy & Jones: 1,625M; UN 1999: 1,650M).

Result: population 0.0252–0.0256% (band 7); GDP 0.034–0.039% (band 8). The 1910 moment gives the same bands (0.024%; 0.033–0.039%).

## Candidate — Window 2, c. 1600 BCE (not fully lawyered)
Gyulavarsánd (Otomani) tell network along the Körös rivers: Békés-Várdomb (Banner & Bóna 1974), Sarkad-Peckesvár, Doboz, Tarhos, Middle Bronze Age levels at Vésztő-Mágor, with satellite open settlements. Density 2–4/km² by analogy with Benta-valley Vatya estimates (Earle & Kristiansen 2010) → 11–22k over 36.2M = 0.03–0.06% population, 0.024–0.073% GDP (×0.8–1.2) → band 8–9. No county-specific published estimate.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 5.** Mezőhegyes imperial-royal military stud (founded 1784 by Joseph II to supply the army with remounts; one of Europe's oldest and largest, 10,000 ha today). Gyula fortress, Ottoman sanjak seat 1566–1695 on the Habsburg–Ottoman frontier. Priscus's account places Attila's camp (449) "between the Tisza and the Körös"; a published local hypothesis puts it at Zsadány. None is a chokepoint or sole command, so no ≥8.
- **Mind 5.** Eponymous Körös culture, the earliest farmers of the Carpathian Basin, with type sites along the Körös inside the polygon; Tessedik's Szarvas agricultural school (1780), among Europe's earliest; Szarvas as geometric centre of the historic Kingdom. Erkel and Munkácsy fall under the event-fame ban.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak rests on the rubric's 5M floor at 4500 BCE. On a Biraben/M&J-interpolated 7M it is 0.16–0.24%; on a HYDE-type ~14M about 0.1%. Band 10 under all three; the extension band moves from 12 to 10.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** No county-specific published population estimate exists for windows 1–2; the densities are regional analogies at the equivalence-rule high end.
- **Boundary smearing.** Pre-1950 censuses cover the 3,670 km² historic county; the ≈118–125k annexed-area reconstruction is mine and should be checked against KSH's restated present-territory series.
- **Double-count risk.** The prehistoric Körös landscape continues into HU321 (Hajdú-Bihar) and HU322 (Jász-Nagykun-Szolnok); the 1950-annexed areas must not be re-counted when HU333, HU321 or HU322 are scored.
- **Not counted.** The Körös culture (6000–5500 BCE) and the Tisza mega-sites (5000–4600 BCE) fall before window 1 and have no denominator in the rubric.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 attained only in window 1; band 7–8 held continuously c. 1860–1941.
