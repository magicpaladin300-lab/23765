# RO422 · Caraș-Severin County (Județul Caraș-Severin), Romania

**UNIT** RO422 (NUTS-3) = Caraș-Severin County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 8,514 km², seat Reșița; municipalities Reșița, Caransebeș; towns Anina, Băile Herculane, Bocșa, Moldova Nouă, Oravița, Oțelu Roșu; 69 communes. Two thirds mountain (Banat, Semenic, Almăj, Țarcu, Godeanu, Poiana Ruscă), with the Timiș–Cerna corridor, the Caraș and Bârzava valleys, the Almăj basin and the upper Danube gorge from Baziaș to Svinița. Historic components: Hungarian Krassó-Szörény county (11,074 km²) less its Lugoj and Făget districts (Timiș) and Orșova (Mehedinți); the Banat Military Frontier's Wallachian-Illyrian regiment (to 1872); the interwar counties of Caraș (Oravița) and the southern half of Severin (Caransebeș). Pre-1948 figures are allocations to the current territory; the INS current-territory series begins in 1948. Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: late Vinča settlement of the Danube terraces (Gornea, Liubcova, Moldova Veche) and the Caraș, Bârzava and Timiș valleys; band 10 at the lawyered top (range 9–10).
**Documented-era peak** Window 4, c. 200 CE: Roman Tibiscum (municipium and customs station), the Herculaneum spa at Ad Mediam, the Banat mines and the Lederata–Tibiscum road; band 9 at the lawyered top (range 7–9; allocation-heavy). Census-grade peak: GDP band 8, 1870–1910, on the StEG mining and metallurgical domain (Reșița, Anina, Oravița, Bocșa); population band 7, 1787–1900.

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 8,514 km² (INS); the mountains (~5,500 km²) and the forested high plateaus are excluded: usable Danube terraces, the Caraș, Bârzava, Timiș and Nera valleys, the Almăj basin and the Bocșa plain fringe 2,000–2,800 km².
2. Cultures at the moment: late Vinča (C–D) on the Danube at Gornea, Liubcova (the Liubcova figurine), Moldova Veche and Pescari, with Vinča–Tiszapolgár contact sites in the Caraș valley and the Timiș corridor; the gorge's early Neolithic (Cuina Turcului, Gornea) is the oldest farming horizon in Romania.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied. → 4,000–8,400, reported 4–8.4k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: farming densities on the Semenic, Almăj and Țarcu; killed any county total above ~11,000 (>4/km² usable).
- Survives: 8,400 people means ~40–80 villages and hamlets of 100–200 on the terraces and in the basins at 5–8 km spacing; cropland need under 3% of the usable area.
- Band 10 holds only at the top of the range (0.168% population, 0.20% GDP); the mid-range is band 9.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Deep score — Window 4, c. 200 CE (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Built numerators: Tibiscum (Jupa, by Caransebeș), municipium with a garrison of ~1,500 (cohors I Vindelicorum, the Palmyrene and Maurish numeri), a station of the Illyrian customs (publicum portorii Illyrici) and extensive civil quarters, 3–6k with its vicus; Ad Mediam–Herculaneum (Băile Herculane), the imperial spa with its governors' and senators' dedications, 1–2k; Praetorium (Mehadia), Arcidava (Vărădia), Centum Putei (Surducu Mare), Berzobis (Berzovia; the camp of legio IIII Flavia Felix in 101–118, afterwards a town site) and the Danube forts 4–8k; the mining vici of the Banat Mountains (Sasca, Moldova Nouă, Ocna de Fier, Dognecea) 3–6k. Towns and forts 11–22k.
2. Countryside: the Timiș–Cerna corridor, the Caraș valley and the Danube bank at 4–8 per km² of lowland, the lawyered reading of Roman Dacia's 0.65–1.2M on ~100,000 km²; 24–43k. County 35–65k.
3. GDP multiplier. Maddison's Roman ratio (~1.2 × world) × role premium 1.1–1.5 relative to the Empire for the customs station, the imperial spa and the mines → 1.3–1.8 (C12); below the 1.6–2.4 of the provincial capitals (Napoca, Romula, Tomis).

Denominator: 190M at 200 CE (McEvedy & Jones).

Result: population 0.018–0.034% (band 7–8); GDP 0.024–0.062% (band 7–9; 9 at the lawyered top; 0.041% at the mid-range of 50k × 1.55).

Veto notes
- Killed: a county above ~75,000 (>9/km² on a two-thirds-mountain polygon); killed a capital-grade multiplier (Tibiscum was never a provincial seat).
- Killed: counting Dierna (Orșova) or Drobeta, both in Mehedinți, or the legion of Berzobis after 118.
- Allocation flag: roughly two thirds of the numerator is a density allocation, so the moment is adopted as the documented-era record on the strength of Tibiscum, the customs station and the mines, with the census-grade 1870–1910 plateau reported beside it.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 7.** Nameable command and chokepoint: the upper Danube gorge (Baziaș–Svinița) and the Roman road and limes along it; the Lederata–Tibiscum invasion road of Trajan's wars and the legionary camp at Berzobis; the Banat Military Frontier regiment at Caransebeș (1768–1872); the Banat mining district under the Treasury (1718) and the StEG (1855), with Reșița, interwar Romania's largest company and builder of its first locomotives; the first railway on present Romanian territory (Oravița–Baziaș, 1854). Not 8: the Iron Gates proper and their dam lie downstream in Mehedinți.
- **Mind 6.** Nameable but regional: the Oravița theatre (1817), the oldest theatre building in Romania; the Herculane spa, a Roman and then Habsburg resort of European fame; the Liubcova figurine and the Cuina Turcului Mesolithic engravings; the Reșița engineering tradition. Nothing of world scale, so 6.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** Band 10 holds only at the top of the Window 1 range (maximum usable area and the 3/km² density together); the mid-range is band 9. The documented-era record (c. 200 CE) is band 9 only at the top of range and band 8 at the mid-range, two thirds of it a density allocation; the census-grade GDP plateau 1870–1910 is a steady band 8, and 1870, 1910 and c. 200 CE have the same mid-range (~0.04%).
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 (~5×); the c. 200 CE GDP figure is ~2.5–3×; the 1870–1910 figures are 1–1.6×.
- **Boundary smearing.** Pre-1948 figures rebuild the polygon from Krassó-Szörény less the Lugoj, Făget and Orșova districts (±15k, 5%), from Krassó county and the frontier regiment before 1872, and from Caraș plus half of Severin in 1930.
- **Double-count risk.** RO424 Timiș (the Lugoj and Făget districts, the Bârzava's lower course), RO413 Mehedinți (Dierna, Orșova, the Iron Gates and their dam), RO423 Hunedoara (Țarcu and the Bistra valley), Serbian Bor and Braničevo (the gorge's right bank, Lederata).
- **Citation flags.** The 2022 county GDP is an estimate carried from the published 2020 figure (the CNP territorial table is on a blocked host); the Hungarian census district tables could not be fetched (mek.oszk.hu blocked), so the 1787–1910 numerators are reconstructions; no printed Roman population estimate for the Banat was retrieved.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1 at the top of range; band 8 held c. 1860–1914 on GDP; band 7 on population 1787–1910.
