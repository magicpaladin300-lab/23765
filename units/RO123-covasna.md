# RO123 · Covasna County (Județul Covasna), Romania

**UNIT** RO123 (NUTS-3) = Covasna County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 3,710 km², seat Sfântu Gheorghe; municipalities Sfântu Gheorghe and Târgu Secuiesc; towns Baraolt, Covasna, Întorsura Buzăului; 40 communes. The eastern half of the Brașov Depression (the Sepsi plain), the Târgu Secuiesc (Kézdi) basin, the Baraolt basin and the Întorsura Buzăului basin, ringed by the Bodoc, Baraolt, Nemira, Vrancea, Buzău and Întorsurii mountains. Historic components: Háromszék County (3,556 km²; 148,080 in 1910), i.e. the Székely seats of Sepsi, Kézdi and Orbai with Miklósvár, less small border adjustments towards Brașov County; the interwar Trei Scaune County. 1950–1968 the territory sat in the Stalin/Brașov Region (polygon not used). Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: the Ariușd (Cucuteni A) group, whose type site lies inside the polygon.
**Documented-era peak** Window 11, 1850 (Willcox floor); band 6, held c. 1850–1930.
**WINNING PATHWAY** GDP in Window 1 (population tied); population in the documented era.

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 3,710 km² (INS); mountains and high hill country take roughly half. Usable: the Sepsi plain, the Kézdi, Baraolt and Întorsura basins and the Olt and Râul Negru valleys, 1,600–1,900 km².
2. Cultures at the moment: the Ariușd group of the Cucuteni A horizon, c. 4600–4300 BCE, named after Ariușd (Erősd) on the Olt; Olteni, Malnaș-Băi, Sfântu Gheorghe-Bedeháza, Reci and Turia are the other published settlements, among the roughly sixty Precucuteni–Ariușd findspots of south-eastern Transylvania. Fortified settlements with large houses and painted pottery on terrace spurs above the Olt.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied. → 3,200–5,700, reported 3–6k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: farming densities on the mountain half; killed any county total above ~7,500 (>4/km² usable).
- Survives: 6,000 people means ~30–60 settlements of 100–200 on the four basins at 5–8 km spacing, consistent with the Ariușd-group site count; cropland need under 3% of the usable area.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 5.** The Oituz pass on the county's eastern edge, the Transylvania–Moldavia gate fought over in 1916–17 (events), and the Székely frontier regiments of 1764–1851 with the second infantry regiment headquartered at Târgu Secuiesc; Gábor Áron's cannon foundry of 1848–49 is an event. A regional pass, so 5.
- **Mind 5.** Ariușd as the type site of the painted-pottery Chalcolithic of south-eastern Transylvania; the Székely National Museum (1875) at Sfântu Gheorghe as the institution of Székely identity; the mineral-water resorts of Covasna, Malnaș and Vâlcele. No claim of more than regional reach.

- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 only (~4–5×).
- **Boundary smearing.** None material; Háromszék and the polygon differ by a few per cent, and the 1850 and 1930 totals are reconstructions within ±5k.
- **Double-count risk.** RO122 Brașov (the western Brașov Depression and the Teutonic Burzenland, scored there), RO124 Harghita (the Székely seats of Udvarhely and Csík), RO211 Bacău (the Oituz pass's eastern side).
