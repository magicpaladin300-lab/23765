# RO221 · Brăila County (Județul Brăila), Romania

**UNIT** RO221 (NUTS-3) = Brăila County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 4,766 km², seat Brăila; towns Făurei, Ianca, Însurăței; 40 communes. The north-eastern Bărăgan steppe, the Buzău and Călmățui valleys, and the Danube floodplain with the Great Brăila Island (≈710 km², marsh until the drainage of the 1960s–70s). Historic components: the interwar Brăila County (seat Brăila; ≈220k in 1930) plus a few communes of the Făurei–Jirlău area from Râmnicu Sărat County. 1950–1968 the territory sat in the Galați Region (polygon not used). Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: Boian–Gumelnița settlement on the Danube, Buzău and Călmățui terraces (Brăilița, Lișcoteanca).
**Documented-era peak** Window 12, 1912: the Danube grain port at its height, with the port export statistics as the named commercial source. Population record 1930 (Window 13), band 6.

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 4,766 km² (INS). The Bărăgan interior was open steppe and the Great Island and Danube floodplain were marsh, so the Chalcolithic settlement zone is the terraces of the Danube, Buzău, Siret and Călmățui and the levees of the floodplain: 2,500–3,500 km² usable.
2. Cultures at the moment: Boian–Gumelnița. Brăilița (Gumelnița settlement with the later Cernavodă I horizon and Bronze Age cemetery) and the Lișcoteanca tells on the Călmățui (Movila Olarului, Moș Filon) are the published anchors; the Brăila Museum's surveys between the Danube, Siret, Buzău and Călmățui record the rest (Harțuche).
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied. → 5,000–10,500, reported 5–10k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: applying farming densities to the Bărăgan steppe and the marsh (another 1,300–2,300 km²), which would add 3–7k with no site evidence behind it.
- Killed: any county total above ~14,000 (>4/km² usable).
- Survives: 10,000 people means ~50–100 villages and hamlets of 100–200 along four river corridors at 5–8 km spacing; cropland need under 3% of the usable area.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Deep score — Window 12, 1912 (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Brăila city 65,053 (1912 census; 58,392 in 1900; 15,767 in 1859; 68,347 in 1930), the Danube's chief grain port and one of the five largest cities of Romania. County on the current territory: 195–210k in 1912, back-projected from the 1930 reconstruction (235–250k: the interwar county's ≈220k plus the Râmnicu Sărat communes) with the Old Kingdom's 1912–1930 growth; 165–185k in 1900.
2. GDP multiplier. Schedule band 2.0–2.5, "quantified fiscal/commercial documentation", named source: the Romanian port and customs export statistics (*Comerțul exterior al României*; the Black Sea port series): Brăila shipped 2,635,450 tons of grain in 1881–1900, 22.2% of Romania's exports by port, level with Galați, and remained the largest grain port until Constanța overtook it after 1910. The port is a third of the county's population, so the entrepôt premium is applied county-wide. Per C12 the premium is relative to Romania, and Romania's ratio to the world is Maddison's 1.14 (Romania $1,741 vs world $1,524 in 1913, the high end of the published Romanian series) → 2.0–2.5 × 1.14 = 2.28–2.85.

Denominator: 1,774M at 1912 (Maddison interpolation between 1900 and 1913).

Result: population 0.0110–0.0118% (band 6); GDP 0.0251–0.0337% (band 7–8; 8 at the lawyered top). The 1900 moment (165–185k over 1,564M, multiplier 2.2–2.75) gives 0.0105–0.0118% and 0.023–0.0325%, the same bands.

Veto notes
- Killed: a Ruling A piercing by valuing the grain shipped through the port at world prices (of the order of 0.04% of world GDP c. 1910). The grain was grown in Teleorman, Ialomița, Buzău and the rest of the hinterland, so counting it here would double-count the producing units; only the port's handling and trading margin is Brăila's, and that is what the schedule premium stands for.
- Killed: Ottoman-era maximalist readings of Ibrail (Evliya Çelebi's house counts) as a numerator for Window 10; the county was 20–40k in 1650 whatever the fortress town held, band 5–7 on Willcox's 470M.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 7.** Nameable commodity: the Danube grain trade. Brăila was Wallachia's chief river port from the fourteenth century, the Ottoman fortress-kaza of Ibrail (1538–40 to 1829; sieges of 1770, 1809 and 1828), a free port from 1836 and, with Galați, the outlet for Romania's wheat exports when Romania was among the world's four largest wheat exporters (c. 1880–1913). Shared with Galați and no chokepoint, so 7.
- **Mind 5.** The cosmopolitan port culture of the free-port century (Greek, Jewish, Bulgarian and Lipovan communities; the radial-concentric town plan of the 1830s; the 1860s theatre). Panait Istrati, Hariclea Darclée, Maria Filotti and Mihail Sebastian fall under the event-fame ban as births.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is the Window 1 floor artefact. The documented-era GDP record rests on the 2.0–2.5 schedule premium for the port; a weighted city/county multiplier (~1.4–1.7) would give 0.016–0.020% and band 7. Band 8 is reached only at the top of the range.
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 only (~4–5×). The 1912 GDP figure is ~2× consensus.
- **Boundary smearing.** All figures before 1948 are reconstructions; the interwar county total and the Râmnicu Sărat communes are ±10k.
- **Double-count risk.** RO224 Galați (the twin port and the Siret boundary), RO222 Buzău and RO226 Vrancea (the Râmnicu Sărat remainder), RO315 Ialomița (the Bărăgan and the grain hinterland). The grain shipped through Brăila is credited to the producing counties, never here.
- **Citation flags.** The 1912 and 1930 county totals on the current territory are reconstructed; the 2022 GDP is inferred from the county's ~0.75% national share.
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1; band 7–8 GDP held c. 1880–1912; band 6 population held 1912–1992.
