# RO116 · Sălaj County (Județul Sălaj), Romania

**UNIT** RO116 (NUTS-3) = Sălaj County.
**Polygon** Current boundaries (Law 2/1968): 3,864 km², seat Zalău; towns Cehu Silvaniei, Jibou, Șimleu Silvaniei; 57 communes. The Silvania hills between the Meseș ridge and the Someș, with the Crasna, Barcău, Agrij and Almaș valleys and the Someș corridor at Jibou. Historic components: most of the Hungarian county of Szilágy (3,815 km²; Kraszna and Közép-Szolnok before 1876) less its Tășnad corner (Satu Mare), plus the Almaș valley from Kolozs and the Someș bend from Szolnok-Doboka; the interwar Sălaj county (5,191 km², with Tășnad and Carei) was larger. Pre-1948 figures are allocations to the current territory; the INS current-territory series begins in 1948. Boundary divergences of one year or less: none material.

**PEAK WINDOW + moment** Window 1 (Chalcolithic), c. 4500 BCE: Iclod–Zau group and Tiszapolgár settlement of the Silvania hills and the Someș, Crasna and Barcău valleys.
**Documented-era peak** Window 4, c. 200 CE: Porolissum (Moigrad–Jac), municipium of the Severan age, capital of Dacia Porolissensis from 124 with a garrison of ~5,000 and a population put at over 15,000, the Meseș Gate limes (Buciumi, Românași, Romita, Tihău) and the frontier market; band 9 at the lawyered top (range 8–9). Census-grade peak: population band 7 (1787–1850), GDP band 7 at the top of range (1850–1910).

## Deep score — Window 1, c. 4500 BCE

Numerator components
1. Area 3,864 km² (INS); the Meseș ridge and the forested Plopiș slopes (~500 km²) and the wettest Someș bottoms are excluded: usable hills and valleys 3,000–3,400 km².
2. Cultures at the moment: the Iclod and Zau groups of the late Transylvanian Neolithic with Tiszapolgár on the Barcău and Crasna; settlements at Zalău (Valea Mîții), Jibou, Șimleu and along the Agrij.
3. Density 2–3 persons/km² on the usable area, the high end of Müller's (2013) regional Neolithic–Chalcolithic densities by the settlement-area method of Zimmermann, Hilpert & Wendt (2009). Equivalence rule applied. → 6,000–10,200, reported 6–10.2k.
4. GDP multiplier 1.0–1.2 (default agrarian).

Denominator: 5.0M at 4500 BCE (rubric floors table; McEvedy & Jones 5M at 5000 BCE, 7M at 4000 BCE).

Veto notes
- Killed: farming densities on the Meseș; killed any county total above ~13,500 (>4/km² usable).
- Survives: 10,200 people means ~50–100 villages and hamlets of 100–200 along the valleys at 4–7 km spacing; cropland need under 3% of the usable area.

Nesting flag (C11): the same stacking across Europe's farmed zone c. 4500 BCE gives a few million Europeans against a 5M world floor, a third to a half of humanity. Floor artefact shared with every farmed Old World unit.

## Deep score — Window 4, c. 200 CE (documented-era peak)

Numerator components
1. Built numerators: Porolissum, the fort of 106 that became the capital of Dacia Porolissensis in 124 and a municipium by the early Severan period, with two forts (Pomăt and Citera), a garrison of ~5,000 auxiliaries, an amphitheatre, baths, temples, a customs station of the Illyrian portorium and a trading town of over 15,000 (the Wikipedia figure, resting on Gudea's excavations); the Meseș Gate forts of Buciumi, Românași, Romita and Tihău with their vici, 4–6k. Town, forts and vici 20–26k.
2. Countryside: the Agrij, Almaș, Crasna and Someș valleys at 5–8 per km² of usable land, the lawyered reading of Roman Dacia's 0.65–1.2M on ~100,000 km² for a frontier zone thick with forts; 15–29k. County 35–55k.
3. GDP multiplier. Maddison's Roman ratio (~1.2 × world) × role premium 1.3–2.0 relative to the Empire for a provincial capital, customs station and frontier market → 1.6–2.4, as for Napoca and Romula (C12).

Denominator: 190M at 200 CE (McEvedy & Jones).

Result: population 0.018–0.029% (band 7–8); GDP 0.030–0.070% (band 8–9; 0.047% at the mid-range of 45k × 2.0; 9 at the lawyered top).

Veto notes
- Killed: a county above ~65,000 (>17/km² usable on a frontier hill country); killed a multiplier above 2.4 (no quantified fiscal source inside the polygon).
- Killed: counting Napoca's territory or the Bologa fort (Cluj).
- Allocation flag: the countryside is a density allocation (two fifths of the numerator); the moment is adopted on the strength of Porolissum's built figure.

## Qualitative dimensions
- **Strategy 6.** Nameable but regional: the Meseș Gate, the north-western gate of Roman Dacia with its limes towers and the Porolissum garrison; the Zalău–Cluj road over the Meseș; the Jibou railway junction; the Șimleu Silvaniei seat of the Báthory family. No chokepoint of continental scale.
- **Mind 6.** Nameable but regional: Porolissum's amphitheatre and customs inscriptions; the Șimleu Silvaniei Gepid treasures (Vienna and Budapest), a reference assemblage for 5th-century goldwork; the Jibou botanical garden; Iuliu Maniu (born at Șimleu, 1873). Nothing of world scale, so 6.

## Flags
- **Ambiguity.** The all-time peak is the Window 1 floor artefact. The documented-era record (c. 200 CE) is band 9 only at the top of range and band 8 at the mid-range; its countryside is a density allocation. Census-grade band 7 is held on population in 1787–1850 and on GDP only at the top of range (1850–1910).
- **Thin lawyering (delta >3×).** Window 1 (~5×) and, at the edge, the c. 200 CE GDP figure (~3×); the 1787–1910 figures are 1–1.4×.
- **Boundary smearing.** Pre-1948 figures rebuild the polygon from Szilágy (less Tășnad, plus the Almaș valley and the Someș bend), from Kraszna and Közép-Szolnok before 1876, and from the interwar county less Tășnad and Carei (±10k, 4%).
- **Double-count risk.** RO113 Cluj (Napoca's territory, the Bologa fort, the Almaș headwaters), RO115 Satu Mare (Tășnad and Carei), RO111 Bihor (the Barcău's lower course), RO114 Maramureș (the Someș bend at Benesat).
- **Citation flags.** Porolissum's "over 15,000" is the encyclopaedic figure; the Hungarian census district tables could not be fetched (mek.oszk.hu is blocked), so the 1787–1910 numerators are reconstructions from the county totals (207,293 in 1900; 230,140 in 1910).
- **Duration metadata (Ruling D).** Band 10 only in Window 1; band 8 held c. 150–250 CE on GDP; band 7 on population 1787–1850.
